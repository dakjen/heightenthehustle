# Connect Your Bank and Put QuickBooks to Work

> Books that answer your questions in a minute: a separate business bank account, QuickBooks Online set up, your bank connected, and the day-one settings checked.

## Why this matters

Books are not paperwork for the tax preparer. They are how you answer business questions without guessing: which months carry the year, what your biggest cost is, whether you can afford to hire. Dee runs a small catering business from a rented kitchen. For two years her records were a box of receipts and a bank statement, handed over once a year so the return could be filed. That got the taxes done. It did not tell her whether January was better than June, or whether the business could carry a second helper. Once her books were set up, those answers took her a minute to find.

## What you'll be able to do

- See your month sorted by category, with profit at the bottom
- Capture every business expense so none of it is taxed as profit
- Run the first rule: a separate bank account for the business
- Start QuickBooks Online Simple Start and connect your bank
- Check the three settings that matter on day one

## The lesson

### Sort the month and the answers appear

Bookkeeping is every dollar in and every dollar out, recorded in categories that mean something. Income from customers minus what the business spends to operate leaves profit, the number the business lives on.

Here is Dee's month, sorted into categories.

| Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Income from customers | $5,200 |
| Food and ingredients | $1,900 |
| Kitchen rental | $600 |
| Packaging | $250 |
| Gas | $180 |
| Insurance | $140 |
| Marketing | $130 |
| **Total expenses** | **$3,200** |
| **Profit** | **$2,000** |

Check it: $1,900 + $600 + $250 + $180 + $140 + $130 = $3,200. Then $5,200 - $3,200 = $2,000. Dee brought in $5,200 and kept $2,000. Because the spending is sorted, she can also see that food is her biggest cost by far. That tells her where a better supplier price or a tighter menu would move profit most. A pile of receipts cannot tell her that.

### Categories pay for themselves

A category, such as Supplies or Insurance, does two jobs. It shows you where the money goes, so you can make decisions. And it matters at tax time, because a business expense you have recorded is a tax deduction: it lowers the profit you are taxed on. Categorized expenses are tax deductions. Forgotten ones are donations to the IRS.

Say Dee pays $40 a month for menu design software and it never gets recorded. Over a year, that is $480 of real business spending that never shows up in her books. Her profit looks $480 higher than it was, and she pays tax on money she already spent. Nobody will ever send her a notice about it. She pays more than she had to, and nothing flags it.

The expenses most likely to slip are the ones paid outside the business account: cash at a supplier, a subscription on a personal card, a purchase reimbursed out of pocket. The system that follows is built to close those gaps.

### The first rule: a separate business bank account

Before any software, there is one rule. The business gets its own bank account. Customer payments go into it. Business costs come out of it. Nothing personal runs through it.

When the account is business-only, every transaction in it is a business transaction by definition. You never have to scroll through a statement asking, "Was that lunch for a client or for me?" It also means every expense is captured without effort, because paying from that account is the record. If your money is still mixed today, opening that account is your first task, ahead of everything else in this unit.

### Choosing and starting QuickBooks

QuickBooks Online comes in several plans. The one called **QuickBooks Online Simple Start** is enough to begin. QuickBooks is a paid subscription. Check current pricing, start with the free trial, and ask whether a program discount is available.

To get started:

1. Sign up for QuickBooks Online Simple Start with your business email.
2. Answer the opening questions about your business. Skip any you are unsure of.
3. Go to the banking area, which may be labeled Banking or Transactions.
4. Choose to connect an account, find your bank, and sign in with your business bank login.
5. Wait while QuickBooks imports your recent transactions.

### The one step that matters most

Connecting your business bank account is the step that makes QuickBooks worth paying for. Once it is connected, transactions import automatically. Every deposit and every charge shows up in QuickBooks on its own, with the date, the amount, and the name of the business.

That changes your job from typing to labeling. You are no longer copying numbers from a bank app into a spreadsheet. You are looking at a list that is already there and telling QuickBooks what each item was. This is why a busy owner can keep the books current in about fifteen minutes a week.

### Three settings to check

Take two minutes in your account and sales settings.

1. **Business name.** Confirm it is spelled the way customers should see it.
2. **Logo.** Upload it so every invoice carries it.
3. **Invoice numbering.** Turn on custom numbering and start at 1001. If you already send invoices, continue from your last number.

### Read the list before you label it

Once the import finishes, scroll the whole list before you categorize anything. You are looking for patterns: which vendors repeat, which kinds of spending are most frequent, and whether anything personal slipped in. Those patterns tell you which categories you will need, which is the work of the next lesson. Five minutes of reading now saves you from building categories you never use.

## Do this now

1. Confirm you have a business-only bank account. If you do not, open one before going further.
2. Start the QuickBooks Online Simple Start free trial. Check current pricing and ask about a program discount first.
3. Connect your business bank account and let transactions import.
4. Check your business name and upload your logo.
5. Set your invoice numbering to start at 1001, or to continue your existing sequence.
6. Scroll through the imported transactions without labeling anything yet. Notice which kinds of spending show up most.

You finish with a QuickBooks account that is connected to your business bank account, shows your real transactions, and carries your name, logo, and invoice numbering.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Business money run through a personal account.** Every month starts with sorting, and deductions get missed. Fix: open a business account and move all business activity into it.
- **An account opened and the bank never connected.** You pay the subscription and still type everything by hand. Fix: connect the bank in the same sitting as you create the account.
- **A bigger plan than the business needs.** The extra features cost money every month and sit unused. Fix: start with Simple Start and move up only when a real need appears.
- **Small expenses left unrecorded.** Each one raises your taxable profit by its full amount. Fix: pay business costs from the business account so they import on their own.
- **Invoices started at number 1.** Fix: start at 1001.

## Before you move on

- Is your business bank account connected, with recent transactions showing in QuickBooks?
- Which business costs are you still paying from a personal card or in cash, and when do they move to the business account?
- What is your biggest category of spending, judging from the imported list?
- Does a test invoice show your business name, your logo, and the right next number?
- Which recurring charge would have gone unrecorded under your old system?

## What you walk away with

A business-only bank account, a QuickBooks account with that bank connected and your key settings checked, and a clear view of where the money goes.
