# Money In, Money Out, and Setting Up QuickBooks

> What bookkeeping actually is, the first rule (a separate business bank account), and getting QuickBooks Online set up with your bank connected.

## Why this matters

Bookkeeping sounds like something you hire out or put off. It is really just knowing where your money came from and where it went. Without it, you are guessing. Dee runs a small catering business from a rented kitchen. For two years she handed her tax preparer a shoebox of receipts and a bank statement, and each year she was told what she owed. She had no idea whether January was better than June, or whether she could afford a second helper. Once her books were set up, those answers took her a minute to find.

## The lesson

### What bookkeeping actually is

Bookkeeping is writing down every dollar in and every dollar out, in categories that mean something. Dollars in are **income**, the money customers pay you. Dollars out are **expenses**, the money the business spends to operate. The gap between them is **profit**, and profit is the number your business lives on.

Here is Dee's month, sorted into categories.

| Category | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Income from customers | $5,200 |
| Food and ingredients | $1,900 |
| Kitchen rental | $600 |
| Packaging | $250 |
| Gas | $180 |
| Insurance | $140 |
| Marketing | $130 |
| **Total expenses** | **$3,200** |
| **Profit** | **$2,000** |

Check it: $1,900 + $600 + $250 + $180 + $140 + $130 = $3,200. Then $5,200 - $3,200 = $2,000. Dee brought in $5,200 and kept $2,000. Because the spending is sorted, she can also see that food is her biggest cost by far. A pile of receipts cannot tell her that.

### Why categories are worth the effort

A **category** is a label for a type of income or expense, such as Supplies or Insurance. Categories do two jobs. First, they show you where the money goes, so you can make decisions. Second, they matter at tax time. A **tax deduction** is a business expense that lowers the profit you are taxed on. Categorized expenses are tax deductions. Forgotten ones are donations to the IRS.

Say Dee pays $40 a month for menu design software and never records it. Over a year, that is $480 of real business spending that never shows up in her books. Her profit looks $480 higher than it was, and she pays tax on money she already spent. Nobody will ever send her a notice about it. She simply pays more than she had to.

### The first rule: a separate business bank account

Before any software, there is one rule. The business gets its own bank account. Customer payments go into it. Business costs come out of it. Nothing personal runs through it.

The reason is simple. When the account is business-only, every transaction in it is a business transaction by definition. You never have to scroll through a statement asking, "Was that lunch for a client or for me?" If your money is still mixed today, opening that account is your first task, ahead of everything else in this unit.

### Choosing and starting QuickBooks

QuickBooks Online is bookkeeping software that runs in your web browser and on your phone. It comes in several plans. The one called **QuickBooks Online Simple Start** is enough to begin. QuickBooks is a paid subscription. Check current pricing, start with the free trial, and ask whether a program discount is available.

To get started:

1. Sign up for QuickBooks Online Simple Start with your business email.
2. Answer the opening questions about your business. Skip any you are unsure of.
3. Go to the banking area, which may be labeled Banking or Transactions.
4. Choose to connect an account, find your bank, and sign in with your business bank login.
5. Wait while QuickBooks imports your recent transactions.

### The one step that matters most

Connecting your business bank account is the step that makes QuickBooks worth paying for. Once it is connected, transactions import automatically. Every deposit and every charge shows up in QuickBooks on its own, with the date, the amount, and the name of the business.

That changes your job from typing to labeling. You are no longer copying numbers from a bank app into a spreadsheet. You are looking at a list that is already there and telling QuickBooks what each item was. This is why a busy owner can keep the books current in about fifteen minutes a week.

### Three settings to check

Take two minutes in your account and sales settings.

1. **Business name.** Confirm it is spelled the way customers should see it.
2. **Logo.** Upload it so every invoice carries it.
3. **Invoice numbering.** Turn on custom numbering and start at 1001. If you already send invoices, continue from your last number.

## Do this now

1. Confirm you have a business-only bank account. If you do not, open one before going further.
2. Start the QuickBooks Online Simple Start free trial. Check current pricing and ask about a program discount first.
3. Connect your business bank account and let transactions import.
4. Check your business name and upload your logo.
5. Set your invoice numbering to start at 1001, or to continue your existing sequence.
6. Scroll through the imported transactions without labeling anything yet. Notice which kinds of spending show up most.

You finish with a QuickBooks account that is connected to your business bank account, shows your real transactions, and carries your name, logo, and invoice numbering.

## Common mistakes

- **Running business money through a personal account.** Fix: open a business account and move all business activity into it.
- **Signing up and never connecting the bank.** Fix: connect the bank in the same sitting as you create the account.
- **Buying a bigger plan than you need.** Fix: start with Simple Start and move up only when a real need appears.
- **Leaving small expenses unrecorded.** Fix: pay business costs from the business account so they import on their own.
- **Starting invoices at number 1.** Fix: start at 1001.
