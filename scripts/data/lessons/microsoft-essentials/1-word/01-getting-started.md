# Word: A One-Page Business Profile That Looks Established

> Produce a one-page profile of your business in Word, with built-in heading styles, one font and even spacing, saved under a name you can find in seconds.

## Why this matters

Sooner or later your business has to put something in writing. A price sheet. A letter to a landlord. A short description of what you do for a grant or a vendor form. People judge the business by how that page looks before they read a word of it. Picture two cleaning companies that send a quote to the same property manager. One is a wall of text in three different fonts. The other has a clear title, short sections, and even spacing. The second one looks like a company that shows up on time. Word is what most offices use for these pages, and three habits carry nearly all of the difference: built-in heading styles, one font, and consistent spacing. This lesson puts those habits to work on a page you will reuse, a one-page profile of your business.

## What you'll be able to do

- Turn plain typed text into a finished page in one formatting pass
- Set one font and size for the whole page in three clicks
- Use Word's heading styles so every section label matches and can be changed all at once
- Keep spacing even from top to bottom without stacking blank lines
- Save the file under a name that tells you the business and the topic at a glance

## The lesson

### Get the words down, then format once

Open Word and choose **Blank document**. Type the whole page before you touch the formatting. Getting the words down plainly and then styling them in one pass is much faster than adjusting each line as you go, and the result is more even.

Word wraps to the next line at the edge of the page, so press **Enter** (Return on a Mac) only to start a new paragraph. To take back your last action, press **Ctrl + Z** on Windows or **Cmd + Z** on a Mac.

Nearly everything you need sits on two tabs of the ribbon, the strip of buttons across the top. **Home** holds the font, size, bold, alignment, and styles. **File** is where you save, open, and print. About six buttons cover this whole lesson.

### One font for the whole page

Mixing fonts is the fastest way to make a page look homemade. One font, used all the way through, looks calm and confident. Calibri, Arial, and Aptos are all safe, readable choices. Body text should be 11 or 12 points, the size number next to the font name.

1. Select all of your text with **Ctrl + A** on Windows or **Cmd + A** on a Mac.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the font name box and choose your font.
3. In the size box next to it, choose 11 or 12.

### Heading styles instead of bold text

Section labels such as "What We Do" are where most business documents go wrong. The common shortcut is to make the label bold and a size bigger. It looks like a heading, but Word still treats it as ordinary text. A built-in heading style gives you three things the shortcut does not. Every heading matches the others automatically. You can change them all at once later. And screen readers, which read documents aloud for people who are blind or have low vision, can tell where each section starts.

1. Click anywhere in the line you want to turn into a heading.
2. On the **Home** tab, find the **Styles** gallery, the row of boxes labeled Normal, Heading 1, Title, and so on.
3. Click **Title** for the name at the top of your page.
4. Click **Heading 1** for each section label.

Leave everything else as **Normal**. Keep bold for a word or two you want to stand out inside a sentence. The shortcut is **Ctrl + B** on Windows or **Cmd + B** on a Mac.

Here is the payoff. To change the look of every heading on the page, right-click **Heading 1** in the Styles gallery and choose **Modify**. Pick a new color or size, click **OK**, and every heading updates together.

### Spacing you set once

Spacing is the room between lines and paragraphs, and the rule is consistency: whatever you do once, do the same way everywhere. Pressing Enter three times in one place and once in another is what makes a page look uneven.

1. Select all of your text.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Line and Paragraph Spacing** button, which looks like lines with up and down arrows.
3. Choose **1.15** for the line spacing.
4. In the same menu, choose **Add Space After Paragraph** if it is offered, so each paragraph gets the same small gap.

### A file name that works for you later

Go to **File > Save As**. Pick a place you will remember, such as your Documents folder or a folder named for your business. Name the file for the business and the topic, like `Marisol-Detailing-About-Us`. Word adds `.docx` to the end, the file type for an editable Word document. After the first save, press **Ctrl + S** on Windows or **Cmd + S** on a Mac every few minutes to save your changes.

## Try it

Use your own business, or use this fictional one: Marisol's Mobile Detailing, a car detailing service that comes to the customer.

1. Open Word and choose **Blank document**.
2. Type the business name on the first line. Example: Marisol's Mobile Detailing.
3. Press Enter and type three section labels, each followed by two or three sentences: **What We Do**, **Who We Serve**, and **How to Reach Us**. Example for the first: "We wash, wax, and deep-clean cars, trucks, and vans at your home or workplace. A standard detail takes about two hours. We bring our own water and power."
4. Select everything and set one font at size 11 or 12.
5. Click in the business name and apply the **Title** style.
6. Click in each section label and apply **Heading 1**.
7. Select everything and set line spacing to 1.15 with the same space after each paragraph.
8. Check that it all fits on one page.
9. Save it with **File > Save As**, using a clear name.

You are done when you have a saved one-page file with a title, three headings built from styles, one font, and even spacing.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Bold, oversized text standing in for headings.** Each one has to be fixed by hand whenever the look changes, and they rarely match. Fix: click the line and choose Heading 1 from the Styles gallery.
- **Two or three fonts on one page.** Fix: select all and set one font, then re-apply your heading styles if needed.
- **Blank lines used to push text down.** They move every time the text above them changes. Fix: delete the extra blank lines and use the spacing button instead.
- **A file saved as "Document1."** It costs you a search every time you need it. Fix: use File > Save As and give it a name with the business and the topic.
- **Formatting line by line while typing.** It doubles the time and leaves the page uneven. Fix: get all the words down, then format in one pass.

## Before you move on

1. Does your profile fit on one page, with the three sections a customer or funder would look for?
2. If you wanted every heading in your brand color, could you change them all with one edit to the Heading 1 style?
3. Is there exactly one font on the page, including any text you pasted in?
4. Could you find this file a month from now by searching your business name?

## What you walk away with

A formatted, saved one-page profile of your business, ready to paste into forms or send on request, plus three habits that carry into everything you write: heading styles, one clean font, and consistent spacing.
