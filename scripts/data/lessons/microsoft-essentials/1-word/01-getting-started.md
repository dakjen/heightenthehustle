# Word: Getting Started

> Open Word, type a one-page document about your business, format it with real headings and one clean font, and save it.

## Why this matters

Sooner or later your business has to put something in writing. A price sheet. A letter to a landlord. A short description of what you do for a grant or a vendor form. People judge the business by how that page looks before they read a word of it. Picture two cleaning companies that send a quote to the same property manager. One is a wall of text in three different fonts. The other has a clear title, short sections, and even spacing. The second one looks like a company that shows up on time. Microsoft Word is the tool most offices use for these pages, and a few simple habits make everything you write in it look professional.

## What you'll learn

- How to open Word, start a blank document, and type in it
- How to change the font and size for the whole page
- How to use real headings instead of just making text bold
- How to keep spacing consistent from top to bottom
- How to save your document with a name you can find later

## The lesson

### Opening Word and finding your way around

Open Word from the Start menu on Windows or from Launchpad or the Dock on a Mac. Choose **Blank document**. You will see a white page with a blinking line on it. That line is the **cursor**. It shows where your typing will appear.

Across the top is the **ribbon**, a wide strip of buttons sorted into tabs. You will use two tabs most of the time. **Home** holds the font, size, bold, alignment, and styles. **File** is where you save, open, and print. You do not need to learn every button. You need about six of them.

### Typing your document

Click on the page and type. Word moves to the next line by itself when you reach the edge, so only press **Enter** (Return on a Mac) when you want to start a new paragraph. If you make a mistake, press **Backspace** (Delete on a Mac) to erase, or undo your last action with **Ctrl + Z** on Windows or **Cmd + Z** on a Mac.

Type everything first. Format second. It is much faster to get the words down plainly and then make them look good than to fuss with each line as you go.

### One clean font

A font is the style of the letters. Pick one easy-to-read font and use it for the whole page. Calibri, Arial, and Aptos are all safe choices. Body text should be 11 or 12 points, which is the size number next to the font name.

1. Select all of your text with **Ctrl + A** on Windows or **Cmd + A** on a Mac.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the font name box and choose your font.
3. In the size box next to it, choose 11 or 12.

Mixing fonts is the fastest way to make a page look homemade. One font, used all the way through, looks calm and confident.

### Real headings, not just bold text

A heading is a short label that tells the reader what a section is about, such as "What We Do." Many people make a heading by making the text bold and bigger. It looks like a heading, but Word still thinks it is ordinary text. A **real heading** uses a built-in style, and that gives you three things. Every heading matches the others automatically. You can change them all at once later. And screen readers, which read documents aloud for people who are blind or have low vision, can tell where each section starts.

1. Click anywhere in the line you want to turn into a heading.
2. On the **Home** tab, find the **Styles** gallery, the row of boxes labeled Normal, Heading 1, Title, and so on.
3. Click **Title** for the name at the top of your page.
4. Click **Heading 1** for each section label.

Leave everything else as **Normal**. Use bold only for a word or two you want to stand out inside a sentence. The shortcut is **Ctrl + B** on Windows or **Cmd + B** on a Mac.

### Consistent spacing

Spacing is the empty room between lines and paragraphs. The rule is simple: whatever you do once, do the same way everywhere. Do not press Enter three times in one place and once in another.

1. Select all of your text.
2. On the **Home** tab, click the **Line and Paragraph Spacing** button, which looks like lines with up and down arrows.
3. Choose **1.15** for the line spacing.
4. In the same menu, choose **Add Space After Paragraph** if it is offered, so each paragraph gets the same small gap.

### Saving your work

Go to **File > Save As**. Pick a place you will remember, such as your Documents folder or a folder named for your business. Give the file a clear name, like `Marisol-Detailing-About-Us`. Word adds `.docx` to the end, which is the file type for an editable Word document. After the first save, press **Ctrl + S** on Windows or **Cmd + S** on a Mac every few minutes to save your changes.

## Try it

Use your own business, or use this fictional one: Marisol's Mobile Detailing, a car detailing service that comes to the customer.

1. Open Word and choose **Blank document**.
2. Type the business name on the first line. Example: Marisol's Mobile Detailing.
3. Press Enter and type three section labels, each followed by two or three sentences: **What We Do**, **Who We Serve**, and **How to Reach Us**. Example for the first: "We wash, wax, and deep-clean cars, trucks, and vans at your home or workplace. A basic detail takes about two hours. We bring our own water and power."
4. Select everything and set one font at size 11 or 12.
5. Click in the business name and apply the **Title** style.
6. Click in each section label and apply **Heading 1**.
7. Select everything and set line spacing to 1.15 with the same space after each paragraph.
8. Check that it all fits on one page.
9. Save it with **File > Save As**, using a clear name.

You are done when you have a saved one-page file with a title, three real headings, one font, and even spacing.

## Common mistakes

- **Faking headings with bold and a bigger size.** Fix: click the line and choose Heading 1 from the Styles gallery.
- **Using two or three fonts on one page.** Fix: select all and set one font, then re-apply your heading styles if needed.
- **Pressing Enter several times to push text down.** Fix: delete the extra blank lines and use the spacing button instead.
- **Saving as "Document1."** Fix: use File > Save As and give it a name with the business and the topic.
- **Formatting while you type.** Fix: get all the words down first, then format in one pass.

A formatted, saved one-page document about your business, plus three habits you will use in everything you write: real headings, one clean font, and consistent spacing.
