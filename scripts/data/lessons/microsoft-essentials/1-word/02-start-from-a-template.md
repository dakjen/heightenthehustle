# Word: Start from a Template

> Open a letter template, replace the placeholder words with your own, and save it as an editable file and a PDF.

## Why this matters

A blank page costs you time. You have to decide where the business name goes, how big it should be, where the date sits, and how to sign off. A template has already made those choices. Say you run a catering business and a church asks for a letter confirming the date, menu, and price for an event. With a template you open the file, type the details, and send it in ten minutes. It carries your business name and contact information at the top, which is called a **letterhead**, and it looks the same every time. That sameness is what makes a small business look established.

## What you'll learn

- What a template is and where to find one
- How to replace placeholder text without breaking the design
- How to keep a clean master copy you can reuse
- How to save a PDF, and why you always send the PDF

## The lesson

### What a template is

A **template** is a document that is already designed. The fonts, colors, spacing, and layout are set. The words are stand-ins, called **placeholder text**, such as "[Your Business Name]" or "Recipient Name." Your job is to replace the stand-ins with real words. You do not redesign anything.

The rule for this course: start from an HTH template instead of a blank page whenever one exists. The template handles the look. You handle the words. You will find the HTH letter template in this lesson's downloads.

### Finding and opening a template

There are two places to get one.

1. **An HTH template.** Download the letter template from this lesson's downloads. Find it in your Downloads folder and double-click it to open it in Word.
2. **A template built into Word.** In Word, go to **File > New**. On a Mac, you may see **File > New from Template**. You will see a set of ready-made designs. Type "letter" or "flyer" in the search box to see more, then click one and choose **Create**.

Flyers work the same way as letters. A flyer template gives you a big headline, a spot for a short message, and a place for your phone number. You replace the words and leave the design alone.

### Save your own copy first

Before you type anything, save the file under a new name. This protects the original.

1. Go to **File > Save As**.
2. Choose your business folder.
3. Name it something like `Letterhead-Master`.

Now fill in the parts that never change: your business name, address, phone, email, and your name and title at the bottom. Save again. This is your **master copy**. Every time you need a new letter, open the master, use **File > Save As** right away with a new name such as `Letter-Grace-Church-2026-04`, and then write that letter. Your master stays clean.

### Replacing placeholder text

Click on a piece of placeholder text. In many templates, one click selects the whole placeholder, and you can just start typing. If it does not select on its own, drag across the words to highlight them, then type. Your new words take on the template's font and size automatically.

Work from the top of the page to the bottom so you do not miss anything.

1. Business name and contact details in the letterhead.
2. The date.
3. The recipient's name and address.
4. The greeting, such as "Dear Ms. Alvarez,".
5. The body, two to four short paragraphs.
6. The closing, your name, and your title.

A good business letter body follows a simple pattern. The first paragraph says why you are writing. The middle gives the details, such as dates, amounts, and what you will provide. The last paragraph says what happens next and how to reach you.

If you paste words from somewhere else and the font suddenly changes, undo it with **Ctrl + Z** on Windows or **Cmd + Z** on a Mac. Then paste again, click the small paste options button that appears, and choose **Keep Text Only**. That makes the pasted words match the template.

### Saving as PDF

A **PDF** is a file that looks the same on every device. A Word file can shift when someone opens it on a phone or on a computer without your fonts. Lines move and pages break in odd places. A PDF is like a printed page on a screen. It is also harder to change by accident. So the rule is: always send the PDF. Keep the Word file for yourself so you can edit it later.

1. Save your Word file first with **Ctrl + S** on Windows or **Cmd + S** on a Mac.
2. Go to **File > Save As**.
3. Find the file type menu. On Windows it is labeled **Save as type**. On a Mac it is labeled **File Format**.
4. Choose **PDF**.
5. Click **Save**.

You now have two files with the same name. The one ending in `.docx` is your editable copy. The one ending in `.pdf` is the one you email or print.

## Try it

Use your own business, or use this fictional one: Golden Spoon Catering, owned by Denise Carter, 410 Maple Street, phone 555-0142.

1. Download the HTH letter template from this lesson's downloads and open it in Word.
2. Use **File > Save As** and name it `Letterhead-Master`.
3. Replace the letterhead placeholders with the business name, address, phone, and email. Replace the signature with your name and title. Save.
4. Use **File > Save As** again and name the new file `Letter-Grace-Church-2026-04`.
5. Write a short letter to a customer. Sample details: Grace Community Church, lunch for 60 guests on Saturday, April 18, baked chicken, rice, green beans, and rolls, total price $900, with a $300 deposit due by April 4.
6. Read it once out loud to catch mistakes. Check that no placeholder text is left.
7. Save the Word file.
8. Use **File > Save As**, choose **PDF** as the file type, and save.

You are done when your folder holds three files: the master letterhead, the editable letter, and the PDF of the letter.

## Common mistakes

- **Typing over the original template.** Fix: use File > Save As with a new name before you type a single word.
- **Leaving placeholder text behind, such as "[Date]".** Fix: read the page top to bottom before saving and look for anything in brackets.
- **Changing the template's fonts and colors.** Fix: leave the design alone and only replace words. If you changed something, undo it.
- **Emailing the Word file.** Fix: save a PDF and attach that instead.
- **Saving only the PDF.** Fix: always keep the editable Word file too, because a PDF is hard to edit later.
