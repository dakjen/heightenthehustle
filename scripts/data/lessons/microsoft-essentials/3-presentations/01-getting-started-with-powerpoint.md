# Getting Started with PowerPoint

> Find your way around PowerPoint, use slide layouts and text boxes, and learn the difference between editing and presenting.

## Why this matters

There will be a day when you have to stand up and explain your business. It may be a vendor fair, a meeting with a property owner, a community group, or a pitch night. Slides give the room something to look at while you talk, and they keep you on track. A home daycare owner speaking to parents at an open house can show her hours, her daily schedule, and her rates on three clean slides instead of reading from a sheet of paper. Before you can design good slides, you need to be comfortable driving the software. This unit covers how to operate the tool and build clean slides. Pitch strategy is covered separately.

## What you'll learn

- The main parts of the PowerPoint window
- How slide layouts and placeholders work
- How to add a text box when you need one
- How to add, rearrange, and delete slides
- How to switch between editing and presenting, and how to save your file

## The lesson

### What PowerPoint is

PowerPoint is Microsoft's program for making presentations. A presentation is a set of **slides**, which are single screens shown one at a time. The whole set is often called a **deck**. Google Slides is Google's version, and it runs in a web browser. You will learn each skill in PowerPoint first, then see how it works in Google Slides.

### The parts of the window

Open PowerPoint and choose **Blank Presentation**. You will see four areas.

- **The ribbon** runs across the top. It is a strip of buttons sorted into tabs such as Home, Insert, Design, Transitions, and Slide Show.
- **The slide pane** is the big area in the middle. This is the slide you are working on right now.
- **The thumbnail pane** is the narrow strip on the left. It shows a small picture of every slide in order.
- **The notes pane** sits below the slide. You can type reminders to yourself there. The audience does not see them.

### Layouts and placeholders

A **layout** is a ready-made arrangement of boxes on a slide. The boxes are called **placeholders**. They say things like "Click to add title" and "Click to add text." Click inside one and type. The placeholder already has a font, a size, and a position, which is why using layouts keeps your slides consistent.

The first slide in a new deck uses the **Title Slide** layout, with one box for a title and one for a subtitle. The layout you will use most is **Title and Content**, which has a title at the top and a larger box below for bullets, a picture, or a chart. Other useful layouts are **Title Only**, **Two Content**, and **Blank**.

To change the layout of a slide you already made:

1. Click the slide in the thumbnail pane.
2. On the **Home** tab, click **Layout**.
3. Choose the layout you want.

### Text boxes

Most of the time, type in the placeholders. Sometimes you need words somewhere a placeholder does not reach, such as a short label under a photo. That is when you add a **text box**.

1. Go to the **Insert** tab and click **Text Box**.
2. Click and drag on the slide to draw the box.
3. Type your words.
4. To move the box, drag its edge. To resize it, drag one of the small circles on its border.

Use text boxes sparingly. Text in placeholders stays consistent from slide to slide. Text in hand-drawn boxes drifts.

### Adding, rearranging, and deleting slides

1. **Add a slide.** On the **Home** tab, click the arrow on **New Slide** and choose a layout. The new slide appears after the one you had selected.
2. **Rearrange slides.** In the thumbnail pane, drag a slide up or down and drop it in its new spot.
3. **Delete a slide.** Click it in the thumbnail pane and press **Delete**.
4. **Undo.** If you delete the wrong one, press **Ctrl + Z** on Windows or **Cmd + Z** on a Mac.

### Editing versus presenting

PowerPoint has two modes, and beginners often mix them up. **Editing** is the normal view you have been using, with the ribbon and the thumbnails. This is where you build. **Presenting** is slide show mode. The slide fills the whole screen and all the tools disappear. This is what your audience sees.

1. Go to the **Slide Show** tab and click **From Beginning**.
2. Press the right arrow key or the spacebar to go to the next slide.
3. Press the left arrow key to go back.
4. Press **Esc** to leave the slide show and return to editing.

You cannot type or fix anything while presenting. If you spot a mistake, press Esc, fix it, and start the show again.

### Saving and reopening

Go to **File > Save As**, choose your business folder, and name the file clearly, such as `Practice-Deck`. PowerPoint adds `.pptx`, the file type for an editable presentation. Save often with **Ctrl + S** on Windows or **Cmd + S** on a Mac. To reopen it, use **File > Open** or double-click the file in its folder.

### In Google Slides

Open Google Slides and choose **Blank presentation**. The layout is nearly the same, with thumbnails on the left and speaker notes below the slide. To add a slide, use **Slide > New slide**. To change a layout, use **Slide > Apply layout**. To add a text box, use **Insert > Text box**. To present, click the **Slideshow** button at the top right, and press Esc to exit. Google Slides saves automatically, so you only need to name the file by clicking **Untitled presentation** at the top left.

## Try it

Use Bright Beginnings Home Daycare, a fictional business, or use your own.

1. Open PowerPoint and choose **Blank Presentation**.
2. On the title slide, type `Bright Beginnings Home Daycare` as the title and `Open House` as the subtitle.
3. Add a new slide with the **Title and Content** layout. Title it `Our Hours`. In the content box, type `Monday to Friday` and `6:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.` on separate lines.
4. Add another **Title and Content** slide. Title it `Our Daily Schedule` and type three lines: `Breakfast and free play`, `Learning time and lunch`, `Nap, snack, and outdoor play`.
5. Save the file as `Practice-Deck`.
6. In the thumbnail pane, drag the schedule slide above the hours slide.
7. Add a fourth slide, then delete it.
8. Start the slide show from the beginning. Click through all three slides and press Esc.
9. Save, close PowerPoint, and reopen the file.

You are done when you have a saved three-slide deck that you reopened and ran in slide show mode.

## Common mistakes

- **Drawing text boxes for everything.** Fix: pick a layout and type in its placeholders.
- **Trying to edit while the slide show is running.** Fix: press Esc to return to editing first.
- **Leaving an empty "Click to add text" box and worrying about it.** Fix: empty placeholders do not show in the slide show, or you can click the border and press Delete.
- **Not knowing which slide is selected.** Fix: look for the highlighted thumbnail on the left before you add or delete.
- **Forgetting to save.** Fix: name the file right away and press Ctrl + S or Cmd + S often.
