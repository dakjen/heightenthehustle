# Build and Run a Three-Slide Deck for an Open House

> Put together a short deck in PowerPoint using layouts, reorder it in seconds, and run it full screen the way an audience will see it.

## Why this matters

There will be a day when you have to stand up and explain your business. It may be a vendor fair, a meeting with a property owner, a community group, or a pitch night. Slides give the room something to look at while you talk, and they keep you on track. A home daycare owner speaking to parents at an open house can show her hours, her daily schedule, and her rates on three clean slides instead of reading from a sheet of paper. This unit covers operating PowerPoint and building clean slides. Pitch strategy is covered separately.

## What you'll be able to do

- Build slides from layouts so every slide matches without manual formatting
- Add a label where a layout does not reach, and know when not to
- Add, reorder, and delete slides in seconds
- Switch between editing and full-screen presenting, and get back out
- Save the deck under a clear name and reopen it, in PowerPoint or Google Slides

## The lesson

### PowerPoint or Google Slides

PowerPoint is Microsoft's presentation program. Google Slides is Google's version, and it runs in a web browser. A presentation is a set of slides, often called a deck. This unit shows each skill in PowerPoint, then gives the Google Slides version at the end of the lesson.

### Where everything is

Open PowerPoint and choose **Blank Presentation**. Four areas do all the work.

- **The ribbon** runs across the top, with buttons sorted into tabs such as Home, Insert, Design, Transitions, and Slide Show.
- **The slide pane** is the big area in the middle, showing the slide you are working on.
- **The thumbnail pane** is the narrow strip on the left, with a small picture of every slide in order.
- **The notes pane** sits below the slide. Reminders you type there are for you. The audience does not see them.

### Let the layout do the formatting

Each slide uses a layout, a ready-made arrangement of boxes. The boxes are called placeholders, and they say things like "Click to add title" and "Click to add text." Click inside one and type. The placeholder already has a font, a size, and a position, which is why slides built from layouts match each other with no extra effort.

The opening slide in a new deck uses the **Title Slide** layout, with one box for a title and one for a subtitle. The layout you will use most is **Title and Content**, which has a title at the top and a larger box below for bullets, a picture, or a chart. Other useful layouts are **Title Only**, **Two Content**, and **Blank**.

![Three slide thumbnails: the first uses the Title Slide layout with a title and subtitle, and the next two use the Title and Content layout with a title on top and a content box below.](/lessons/microsoft-essentials--u3-01-getting-started-with-powerpoint.svg)

To change the layout of a slide you already made:

1. Click the slide in the thumbnail pane.
2. On the **Home** tab, click **Layout**.
3. Choose the layout you want.

### A text box for the exceptions

Most of the time, type in the placeholders. Sometimes you need words somewhere a placeholder does not reach, such as a short label under a photo. That calls for a text box.

1. Go to the **Insert** tab and click **Text Box**.
2. Click and drag on the slide to draw the box.
3. Type your words.
4. To move the box, drag its edge. To resize it, drag one of the small circles on its border.

Use text boxes sparingly. Text in placeholders stays consistent from slide to slide. Text in hand-drawn boxes drifts.

### Add, reorder, and delete slides

1. **Add a slide.** On the **Home** tab, click the arrow on **New Slide** and choose a layout. The slide appears after the one you had selected.
2. **Rearrange slides.** In the thumbnail pane, drag a slide up or down and drop it in its new spot.
3. **Delete a slide.** Click it in the thumbnail pane and press **Delete**.
4. **Undo.** If you delete the wrong one, press **Ctrl + Z** on Windows or **Cmd + Z** on a Mac.

### Editing and presenting are separate modes

PowerPoint has two modes. Editing is the normal view, with the ribbon and the thumbnails, and it is where you build. Presenting is slide show mode. The slide fills the whole screen, the tools disappear, and this is what your audience sees.

1. Go to the **Slide Show** tab and click **From Beginning**.
2. Press the right arrow key or the spacebar to go to the next slide.
3. Press the left arrow key to go back.
4. Press **Esc** to leave the slide show and return to editing.

You cannot type or fix anything while presenting. If you spot a mistake, press Esc, fix it, and start the show again.

### Save and reopen

Go to **File > Save As**, choose your business folder, and name the file clearly, such as `Practice-Deck`. PowerPoint adds `.pptx`, the file type for an editable presentation. Save often with **Ctrl + S** on Windows or **Cmd + S** on a Mac. To reopen it, use **File > Open** or double-click the file in its folder.

### In Google Slides

Open Google Slides and choose **Blank presentation**. The layout is nearly the same, with thumbnails on the left and speaker notes below the slide. To add a slide, use **Slide > New slide**. To change a layout, use **Slide > Apply layout**. To add a text box, use **Insert > Text box**. To present, click the **Slideshow** button at the top right, and press Esc to exit. Google Slides saves automatically, so you only need to name the file by clicking **Untitled presentation** at the top left.

## Try it

Use Bright Beginnings Home Daycare, a fictional business, or use your own.

1. Open PowerPoint and choose **Blank Presentation**.
2. On the title slide, type `Bright Beginnings Home Daycare` as the title and `Open House` as the subtitle.
3. Add a slide with the **Title and Content** layout. Title it `Our Hours`. In the content box, type `Monday to Friday` and `6:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.` on separate lines.
4. Add another **Title and Content** slide. Title it `Our Daily Schedule` and type three lines: `Breakfast and free play`, `Learning time and lunch`, `Nap, snack, and outdoor play`.
5. Save the file as `Practice-Deck`.
6. In the thumbnail pane, drag the schedule slide above the hours slide.
7. Add a fourth slide, then delete it.
8. Start the slide show from the beginning. Click through all three slides and press Esc.
9. Save, close PowerPoint, and reopen the file.

You are done when you have a saved three-slide deck that you reopened and ran in slide show mode.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Text boxes drawn for everything.** Fix: pick a layout and type in its placeholders.
- **Trying to fix a slide while the show is running.** Fix: press Esc to return to editing, make the change, and restart.
- **Time spent removing empty "Click to add text" boxes.** Empty placeholders do not show in the slide show. Fix: leave them, or click the border and press Delete.
- **Adding or deleting with the wrong slide selected.** Fix: look for the highlighted thumbnail on the left before you add or delete, and undo if needed.
- **A deck that exists only as an unsaved window.** Fix: name the file right away and press Ctrl + S or Cmd + S often.

## Before you move on

1. Could you add a rates slide to your deck and move it to second position in under a minute?
2. Are all of your slides built from layouts, with text boxes used only where a layout could not do the job?
3. Can you start the show, move forward and back, and exit using only the keyboard?
4. Have you seen every slide at full screen before anyone else does?

## What you walk away with

A saved three-slide deck built from layouts, and a routine for every deck after it: build, reorder, check at full screen, save.
