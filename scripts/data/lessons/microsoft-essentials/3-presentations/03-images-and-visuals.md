# Working with Images and Visuals

> Insert and resize pictures without stretching them, add simple shapes and a chart, and use only images you have permission to use.

## Why this matters

People remember what they see far better than what they read on a screen. A photo of a finished job does more than three bullet points describing it. A chart shows where the money goes faster than a row of numbers. For a caterer, one clear photo of a plated meal sells the food. For a landscaper, a before-and-after pair tells the whole story. Visuals can also hurt you. A stretched logo, a blurry photo, or a picture copied from someone else's website makes a business look careless, and the last one can bring a bill or a legal complaint. This lesson covers adding visuals the right way.

## What you'll learn

- How to insert a picture and resize it without distortion
- How to crop a picture to show only the part you want
- How to add a simple shape
- How to bring in a chart from your spreadsheet
- How to tell which images you have permission to use

## The lesson

### Inserting a picture

1. Click the slide where you want the picture.
2. Go to the **Insert** tab and click **Pictures**.
3. Choose **This Device** on Windows or **Picture from File** on a Mac.
4. Find the picture on your computer, click it, and click **Insert**.

The picture lands on the slide with small circles around its border. Those circles are called **handles**. To move the picture, click in the middle of it and drag.

### Resizing without distortion

**Distortion** means the picture is stretched or squashed so people look too wide or too tall. It happens when you drag a handle on the side, top, or bottom of a picture. Those handles change only one direction.

The fix is simple: always resize from a **corner handle**. A corner changes the width and height together, so the picture keeps its shape. Holding **Shift** while you drag a corner locks the shape for certain. If you stretch a picture by accident, undo with **Ctrl + Z** on Windows or **Cmd + Z** on a Mac.

Two more tips. Do not enlarge a small picture to fill the slide, because it will turn blurry. Start with a large, sharp photo and make it smaller. And leave some room around the picture. It should not touch the edges of the slide or crowd the title.

### Cropping

**Cropping** trims away the outer parts of a picture, the way you might cut a printed photo with scissors. Use it to remove a messy background or to focus on what matters.

1. Click the picture.
2. Go to the **Picture Format** tab, which appears when a picture is selected.
3. Click **Crop**. Black marks appear at the edges of the picture.
4. Drag a black mark inward to trim that side.
5. Click outside the picture to finish.

### Simple shapes

Shapes are useful in small doses. A rectangle behind text makes it readable on top of a photo. An arrow points to a detail. A colored bar under a title ties slides together.

1. Go to the **Insert** tab and click **Shapes**.
2. Click a shape, such as a rectangle or an arrow.
3. Click and drag on the slide to draw it.
4. With the shape selected, use the **Shape Format** tab to change its fill color.

Keep shapes plain and use the colors you already chose for the deck. One arrow helps. Six arrows and a starburst do not.

### Adding a chart

A chart turns numbers into a picture. The easiest way to get one onto a slide is to copy the chart you already built in Excel.

1. Open your Excel workbook and click the chart once to select it.
2. Copy it with **Ctrl + C** on Windows or **Cmd + C** on a Mac.
3. Switch to PowerPoint and click the slide.
4. Paste it with **Ctrl + V** on Windows or **Cmd + V** on a Mac.
5. Resize it from a corner handle.
6. Check that the labels are large enough to read from across a room. If not, click the text in the chart and raise the font size on the **Home** tab.

If you do not have a chart yet, go to **Insert > Chart**, choose **Column**, and click **OK**. A small grid opens. Replace the sample numbers with your own and close the grid.

Give the chart slide a title that states the point, such as `Rent Is Our Largest Cost`. A title that tells people what to notice is better than one that just says "Chart."

### Images you have permission to use

Finding a picture online does not mean you are allowed to use it. Most photos belong to the person who took them, and using one without permission can lead to a demand for payment. These are safe choices.

- **Photos you took yourself** of your own work, shop, or products.
- **Photos you paid a photographer for**, as long as your agreement lets you use them for your business.
- **Stock photos with a license that allows business use.** A license is the set of rules for how you may use a picture. Read it before you download. If your version of PowerPoint shows **Stock Images** under **Insert > Pictures**, those are included for use in your slides.
- **Your own logo.**

Be careful with photos of people. Ask customers before you show their faces, their homes, or their cars. If you work with children, get written permission from a parent or guardian before using any photo of a child.

### In Google Slides

To insert a picture, go to **Insert > Image > Upload from computer**. Resize from a corner handle the same way. To crop, double-click the picture and drag the black marks. For shapes, go to **Insert > Shape**. To bring in a chart from Google Sheets, go to **Insert > Chart > From Sheets** and choose your spreadsheet.

## Try it

You need one photo of your own business, such as your work, your space, or a product. If you do not have one yet, take one now with your phone and send it to your computer. You also need the `March Expenses` chart from your `Budget-Tracker` workbook.

1. Open PowerPoint, start a blank presentation, and save it as `Visuals-Practice`.
2. Add a **Title and Content** slide. Title it with a short phrase that describes your photo, such as `Fresh Cuts Every Day`.
3. Insert your photo. Resize it from a corner so it fills about half the slide.
4. Crop out anything distracting at the edges.
5. On purpose, stretch the photo from a side handle. See how it looks, then undo.
6. Insert a rectangle shape, make it one of your colors, and place it as a thin bar under the title.
7. Add another slide. Title it `Rent Is Our Largest Cost`.
8. Copy the chart from Excel and paste it onto this slide. Resize it from a corner and check that the labels are readable.
9. Save the file.

You are done when you have a saved two-slide deck with one undistorted photo you have the right to use and one chart from your spreadsheet.

## Common mistakes

- **Dragging a side handle to resize.** Fix: undo, then drag from a corner.
- **Blowing up a small picture until it is blurry.** Fix: use a larger original or keep the picture small.
- **Copying a photo from a search result.** Fix: use your own photo or one with a license that allows business use.
- **Chart labels too small to read.** Fix: select the chart text and raise the font size.
- **Too many visuals on one slide.** Fix: one photo or one chart per slide, with a title that says what to notice.
