# Presenting and Sharing Your Work

> Run your deck smoothly in front of a room with presenter notes and one quiet transition, then send a clean PDF afterward.

## Why this matters

A finished deck is only half the job. You still have to run it in front of people, and you still have to get it to them afterward. Both are places where things go wrong. The slide show will not start. The notes you wrote are nowhere to be seen. The file you emailed opens with scrambled fonts on the other person's phone. A food truck owner presenting to a market committee has a few minutes to make a good impression. Time spent wrestling with the laptop comes out of those minutes. Knowing exactly how to start the show, move through it, and send a clean copy afterward means the committee remembers the food and not the technical trouble.

## What you'll be able to do

- Start, move through, and end a slide show from the keyboard on any computer
- Keep your talking points in presenter notes that only you see
- Apply one quiet transition to the whole deck in four clicks
- Export a PDF that opens correctly on any device
- Rehearse, collect useful feedback, and run a pre-event technical check

## The lesson

### Run the show from the keyboard

Slideshow mode fills the screen with one slide at a time. It is what the audience sees.

1. Go to the **Slide Show** tab.
2. Click **From Beginning** to start at slide 1. Click **From Current Slide** to start at the slide you have selected.
3. Press the **right arrow** key or the **spacebar** to move forward.
4. Press the **left arrow** key to go back.
5. Press **Esc** to end the show.

On Windows, **F5** also starts the show from the beginning. On a Mac, the shortcut is **Cmd + Shift + Return**. Commit the arrow keys and Esc to memory. With those three keys you can run any presentation on any computer.

### Presenter notes

Presenter notes are reminders you type for yourself in the notes pane below each slide in editing view. The audience never sees them. Notes let you keep the slide short while keeping the full details close at hand.

1. Click a slide.
2. Click in the notes pane below it. If you do not see it, click **Notes** at the bottom of the window.
3. Type what you want to remember to say. Write short phrases, not a full script.

A good note for a slide titled `What We Offer` might read: "Mention the kids' Saturday special. Walk-ins welcome before noon. Products are by the register."

To see your notes while you present, use **Presenter View**. On the **Slide Show** tab, check the box for **Use Presenter View**. When your laptop is connected to a projector or second screen, the audience sees only the slide. Your laptop shows the current slide, your notes, and the next slide. If you have only one screen, print your notes instead. Go to **File > Print** and choose **Notes Pages** in the layout options.

### One quiet transition

A transition is the effect that plays when one slide changes to the next. The best transition is one nobody notices. Choose one quiet effect and use it on every slide.

1. Click any slide.
2. Go to the **Transitions** tab.
3. Click **Fade**.
4. Click **Apply To All**.

That is all you need. Skip the spinning, bouncing, and checkerboard effects. They slow you down and pull attention away from what you are saying. If you ever want to remove transitions, choose **None** and click **Apply To All**.

### Export a PDF to send

You should end up with two versions of every deck. The `.pptx` file is your editable copy. Keep it so you can update the deck later. The PDF is the copy you send to other people. A PDF looks the same on every phone, tablet, and computer, even if the person does not have PowerPoint. It also cannot be changed by accident. Transitions do not play in a PDF, and each slide becomes one page.

1. Save your PowerPoint file.
2. Go to **File > Save As**. On a Mac, you can also use **File > Export**.
3. In the file type menu, choose **PDF**.
4. Click **Save** or **Export**.
5. Open the PDF and page through it to make sure every slide looks right.

Send the PDF when someone needs to read your slides. Send the PowerPoint file only when someone needs to edit them with you.

### Rehearse and get feedback

Run through your deck out loud at least twice before the real thing. Stand up. Use the arrow keys. Time yourself. For five slides, aim for two to three minutes.

Then present to one other person and ask for feedback on the things this unit covers, which are readability and technical execution. Give them these questions.

1. Could you read every word from where you sat?
2. Did any slide have too much on it?
3. Did the slides look like they belonged together?
4. Did the show start, move, and end smoothly?
5. Was any picture blurry or stretched?

Write down what they say and fix those things in the editable file. Then export a fresh PDF so both versions match.

### The pre-event technical check

Before any real presentation, do a quick technical check. Bring your charger. Carry the PDF on a USB drive or in your email as a backup. If you can, arrive early and test the projector.

### In Google Slides

Click the **Slideshow** button at the top right to present. Click the small arrow next to it and choose **Presenter view** to see your notes in a separate window. Speaker notes go in the box below each slide. For a transition, go to **Slide > Transition**, choose **Fade** in the panel, and click **Apply to all slides**. To make a PDF, go to **File > Download > PDF Document**. To get a PowerPoint copy, choose **Microsoft PowerPoint** in the same menu.

## Try it

Open the five-slide deck you built in the last lesson.

1. Add presenter notes to every slide. Two or three short reminders each is enough.
2. Go to the **Transitions** tab, choose **Fade**, and click **Apply To All**.
3. Start the show from the beginning. Move through all five slides with the arrow keys and exit with Esc.
4. Practice out loud twice and time yourself.
5. Present to a classmate, friend, or family member. Ask them the five feedback questions above.
6. Fix at least one thing based on what they told you.
7. Save the PowerPoint file.
8. Export a PDF with the same name.
9. Open the PDF and confirm it has five pages and looks right.

You are done when your folder holds two matching files: the editable `.pptx` deck and the PDF.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Reading the slides word for word.** Fix: keep slides short and put the details in your notes.
- **A different flashy transition on every slide.** Fix: choose Fade and click Apply To All.
- **Emailing only the PowerPoint file.** Fix: send the PDF, which opens the same way on any device.
- **Editing the deck after exporting, with no fresh export.** Fix: make a fresh PDF every time you change the editable file.
- **Meeting the room's setup at the moment you are due to speak.** Fix: rehearse with the arrow keys beforehand, arrive early, and carry a backup copy.

## Before you move on

1. Can you start your deck, move both directions, and end it without touching the trackpad?
2. Do your notes hold the details, such as specials and prices, so the slides can stay short?
3. Is the PDF in your folder exported after your last edit, and does it have the right number of pages?
4. What is your backup if the venue's equipment will not open your file?

## What you walk away with

You can run a presentation on your own, use notes and one quiet transition, and share your work as a clean PDF while keeping an editable copy for next time.
