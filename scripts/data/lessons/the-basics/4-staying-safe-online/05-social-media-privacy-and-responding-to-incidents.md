# Social Media Privacy and Responding to Incidents

> Control what the public can see, keep personal and business separate, handle impersonation, and recover an account that gets taken over.

## Why this matters

For many small businesses, a social media page is the storefront. It is where customers find you, see your work, and send you messages. That makes it valuable to you and to scammers. Imagine a hair braider whose business page gets taken over on a Friday. The thief posts a fake "deposit special" and collects payments from her regular customers all weekend. She loses money, bookings, and some of the trust she spent years building. Careful privacy settings lower the chance of this happening, and a clear plan shortens the damage if it does. Your reputation is a business asset, and it deserves the same protection as your cash.

## The lesson

### Privacy settings

**Privacy settings** control who can see your posts, your profile details, your friends list, and your contact information, and who can message or tag you. Every platform arranges them differently and changes them from time to time, but they are always found under **Settings**, usually in a section called **Privacy** or **Account**. Many platforms also offer a privacy checkup that walks you through the main choices.

For a personal account, look at five things: who can see your posts, who can see your phone number and email, who can see your friends or followers list, who can send you messages, and whether your profile shows up in search engines. Choosing "friends" or "followers you approve" in place of "public" is a sound starting point. Also remove details you do not need to share, such as your full birth date and home address. Scammers use those details to guess passwords, answer security questions, and write convincing messages to the people you know.

### Location sharing

Location shows up in three ways: a tag you add to a post, hidden location details saved inside a photo, and live location sharing in apps. For a business with a storefront, sharing your shop's address is good marketing. Sharing your home address is not. This matters most if you run your business from home, as many childcare providers, bakers, and cleaners do.

Posting "At the beach all week!" in real time also tells the public that your home or shop is empty. A simple habit is to post trip photos after you are back. In your phone's Settings, under **Privacy** or **Location**, you can choose which apps may use your location and turn it off for the camera and for social media apps that do not need it.

### Keeping personal and business separate

Use a business page or business profile for your business, and keep your personal account personal. Most platforms offer a business account type, often with extra tools such as business hours, contact buttons, and insights about your posts. Sign up for business accounts with your business email address and a business phone number where you can, so your personal contact details are not on display.

Separation protects both sides. Customers do not need to see your family photos, your children's school, or your personal opinions, and a problem on one account is less likely to spread to the other. If the platform lets you add other people to manage a business page, add them through its official roles feature. Do not share your password. Roles let you remove someone's access in one click when they leave. Having a second trusted person with a role on the page also gives you a way back in if your own account is locked.

### Impersonation

**Impersonation** is when someone copies your name, logo, and photos to create a fake account, then messages your followers. They may announce fake giveaways, ask for deposits, or send links that steal sign-in details. Signs include customers saying they got a strange message from you, a second account with your name and a slightly different spelling, or a new account with your photos and very few followers.

When it happens, do not argue with the fake account. Take screenshots of the profile and its messages. Report the account using the platform's own report option, which usually includes a choice such as "pretending to be someone" or "impersonation." Ask a few customers and friends to report it too. Then post a short notice on your real page telling customers which account is yours and that you will never ask for payment through a direct message from a new account.

### If an account is compromised

A **compromised** account is one someone else has gotten into. Warning signs include posts or messages you did not send, a password that suddenly does not work, emails about a changed password or email address that you did not request, or sign-in alerts from places you have never been.

1. **If you can still sign in,** change the password right away to a new, unique one. Then look in the security settings for an option to sign out of all other devices, and use it.
2. **If you cannot sign in,** use the official recovery option on the platform's own sign-in page, such as "Forgot password" or "My account was hacked." Reach it through the app or by typing the platform's address yourself.
3. Secure the email account connected to it. Change that password too and confirm two-step verification is on.
4. Turn on two-step verification on the recovered account.
5. Check the account settings for anything the intruder changed: the recovery email and phone number, connected apps, page roles, and any payment or advertising details. Remove what you do not recognize.
6. Delete posts and messages the intruder sent, and tell your customers what happened and what to ignore.
7. If a card or bank account was linked, call your bank. Report the incident to the platform, and to the Federal Trade Commission if money was lost.

One warning about recovery: people who contact you offering to get your account back for a fee are scammers, and so are most "recovery services" advertised in comment sections. Platforms do not charge to recover an account. Use only the platform's official help pages.

## Common mistakes

- **Running the business from a personal profile.** Set up a business page or business account and move customers there.
- **Posting your location in real time.** Share trips and events after they are over, and never tag your home.
- **Sharing your password with someone who helps with posts.** Add them through the platform's roles feature.
- **Paying someone who promises to recover your account.** Use only the official recovery process.
- **Recovering the account but not checking the settings.** Review recovery contacts, connected apps, and roles so the intruder cannot come back.
