# Stay Connected and Land on the Official Site Every Time

> Get back online in minutes when the connection drops, and reach the real licensing, tax, and banking sites without paying a middleman.

## Why this matters

Licenses, permits, tax accounts, supplier catalogs, and loan applications have nearly all moved online. With a reliable connection and the right site saved, you can handle business from your shop, your van, or your kitchen table. Think of a home repair contractor who renews a license between jobs. He joins a network he trusts, goes straight to the licensing office's own site from a bookmark, and is done in fifteen minutes. A competitor who clicks the top search result may land on a look-alike site and pay a middleman an extra fee for something the real office does directly.

## What you'll be able to do

- Get a laptop or phone onto a trusted network and confirm the connection is live
- Work through a seven-step check that restores most dropped connections without a service call
- Use your phone's data plan as a backup when Wi-Fi fails mid-job
- Tell an official site from an ad or a look-alike before you click
- Bookmark your bank, licensing office, and suppliers so you reach the real site in one click
- Download a form and file it where you can find it

## The lesson

### Join a network you trust

1. Open **Settings** and choose **Wi-Fi** or **Network and Internet**. On a computer, you can also click the Wi-Fi symbol in the corner of the screen.
2. Make sure Wi-Fi is turned on.
3. Choose the network name from the list. Pick only networks you know and trust, such as your home, your shop, or the classroom network.
4. Type the password exactly. Capital and lowercase letters count.
5. Wait for the word **Connected** or a check mark next to the network name.

The Wi-Fi symbol in the corner of the screen, with its lines filled in, confirms you are connected. Open networks at coffee shops and markets are convenient, but you do not control them. Save banking, tax accounts, and anything with a password for a network you control.

### Keep a backup connection

Your phone also reaches the internet through cellular data from your phone plan, with no Wi-Fi needed. That is why the phone works in the parking lot while the laptop does not. When the shop's Wi-Fi drops in the middle of a job, switch the phone off Wi-Fi and finish the urgent task on cellular data.
### When the connection drops: seven checks

You will know there is a problem when pages never finish loading, a message such as "No internet" or "You are offline" appears, or the Wi-Fi symbol shows an X, a slash, or an exclamation mark. Work through these checks in order, from quickest to biggest.

1. Check that Wi-Fi is on and that airplane mode is off. Airplane mode turns off all wireless connections.
2. Check that you are on the right network, not a neighbor's.
3. Turn Wi-Fi off, wait a few seconds, and turn it back on.
4. Move closer to the router, the box your internet service comes through. Walls and distance weaken the signal.
5. Restart your device.
6. Unplug the router, wait about thirty seconds, and plug it back in. Give it a few minutes to start.
7. Try another device. If nothing in the building can connect, the problem is with your internet service, and it is time to call your provider.

Most problems end at step three or five.
### Work in tabs, save with bookmarks

Tabs let you keep several pages open at once in your browser, whether that is Chrome, Safari, Edge, or Firefox. Open a supplier's price list in one tab and your order form in another, and switch between them instead of losing your place. Click the plus sign at the top to open a tab, click a tab to switch to it, and click the X to close it. On a phone, look for a small square or a number near the address bar to see your tabs.
A bookmark (some browsers say favorite) saves a page so you return with one click. Look for a star or a bookmark shape near the address bar, or open the browser's menu and choose to add a bookmark. Bookmark the pages you use for business: your bank, your licensing office, your tax account, your suppliers. A bookmark also protects you, because it always takes you to the real site you saved, not to whatever a search turns up that day.

### Reach the official site, not a look-alike

Typing a full web address into the address bar takes you straight to that site. Typing plain words runs a search and returns a list of pages that might match. In most browsers the address bar does both jobs. Search is the right tool when you do not know the address, but read the results before you click.

- The top few results are often ads, marked with a small word such as "Sponsored" or "Ad." They are not always the official site.
- Look at the address shown under the result's title. Government offices in the United States generally use addresses ending in .gov.
- If a site charges a fee to file something a government office handles directly, stop and check the address again.

### Download a form and file it

Many offices post forms as PDF files. Click the link or the download button, usually marked with a downward arrow. The browser shows the download in a small panel, and the file lands in your Downloads folder. Rename it and move it to your business folder, as you did in the last lesson.

## Try it

1. Open Wi-Fi settings and confirm you are connected to an approved network. If you are in class, use the classroom network.
2. Turn Wi-Fi off, watch the symbol change, and turn it back on.
3. Open your browser and use search to find the official website for business licenses or registration in your city, county, or state.
4. Check the address under the result before you click. Skip anything marked as an ad.
5. Bookmark the page.
6. Open a second tab and find another resource, such as your local small business development office. Switch between the two tabs.
7. Download the sample form from this lesson's downloads, then move it into your business folder. You now have a working connection, one bookmarked business resource, and a downloaded form filed where you can find it.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Clicking the top search result without reading it.** This is how owners end up paying a middleman. Check for the "Sponsored" label and read the address before you click.
- **Joining any open network that shows up.** Connect only to networks you know and trust, and keep banking off public Wi-Fi.
- **Retyping the Wi-Fi password in a hurry.** Capital letters count. Type it slowly once instead of quickly three times.
- **Keeping dozens of tabs open.** The device slows down and the page you need gets buried. Close what you are done with and bookmark what you need to keep.
- **Calling the provider before running the checks.** You wait on hold, and they walk you through the same seven steps. Run them yourself first.

## Before you move on

- If the shop's Wi-Fi dropped right now with a quote due, what would you do in the next two minutes to get it sent?
- Do you know where your router is and how to restart it?
- Are your bank, your licensing office, and your tax account bookmarked on the device you use most?
- Can you point to the part of a search result that tells you whether a site is official?

## What you walk away with

You can get online and stay online, restore a dropped connection on your own before calling anyone, fall back on your phone's data when you have to, and reach the official sites your business depends on in one click.
