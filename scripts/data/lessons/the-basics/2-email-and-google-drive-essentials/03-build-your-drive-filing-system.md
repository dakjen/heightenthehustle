# Build a Drive Filing System That Finds Anything in Ten Seconds

> One master business folder, five drawers, and a file-naming rule that sorts itself, so you can say yes the same day someone asks for a document.

## Why this matters

Sooner or later, someone asks you for a document, and they want it today. A lender asks for last year's tax return. A customer says they never got an invoice. A grant application wants your business license by 5 p.m. With files scattered across a phone, an email account, a laptop desktop, and a shoebox, that request turns into a lost afternoon. A childcare provider with her license, insurance, and inspection report in one organized place can apply for a grant in the time it takes someone else to locate their paperwork. A filing system is what lets you say yes quickly.

## What you'll be able to do

- Keep business records in Google Drive so they outlast any one phone or laptop
- Build one master business folder with five numbered drawers
- Name every file so it sorts itself and shows up in search
- Scan paper receipts and letters into the right folder from your phone
- Keep the system current with a one-minute daily habit

## The lesson

### Why Drive, and why drawers come before files

Google Drive is the online storage that comes with your Gmail account. Files you put in Drive live in your Google account, not on one device, so you can open them from your phone, your laptop, or a library computer by signing in. If your phone breaks or your laptop is stolen, your files are still there. You reach Drive from the grid of Google apps in the corner of Gmail, or through the Drive app on your phone.

Treat Drive as a filing cabinet. A cabinet only works with labeled drawers, and the rule is this: build the drawers before you upload. Fifty files dumped in to be sorted later tend to stay unsorted.

### One master folder, five drawers

Start with one master folder named after your business. Everything about the business goes inside it, and nothing else does. Inside the master folder, create five folders:

```
Johnson Cleaning Co
  01 Clients
      Johnson, Mary
      Reed Law Office
  02 Money
      2025
      2026
  03 Legal & Admin
  04 Marketing
  05 Operations
```

The numbers in front keep the drawers in the same order every time, so your eyes always know where to look. Here is what goes in each one.

- **01 Clients.** One folder per client. Their quotes, agreements, photos of the job, and anything they send you.
- **02 Money.** Invoices, receipts, bank statements, and taxes, with a folder for each year. At tax time, you hand over one year's folder.
- **03 Legal & Admin.** Your business license, registration papers, tax ID letter, insurance, permits, and contracts with vendors.
- **04 Marketing.** Your logo, photos, flyers, social media graphics, and your business overview.
- **05 Operations.** How you run the work: price lists, checklists, schedules, supplier lists, and templates.

If you are not sure where a file goes, ask who or what it is about. About one customer? Clients. About dollars in or out? Money. Proof the business is legal? Legal & Admin.

### Build the folders

1. Open Drive and click the New button.
2. Choose New folder, type your business name, and create it.
3. Double-click the master folder to open it.
4. Click New, then New folder again, and create "01 Clients." Repeat for the other four.
5. Open 02 Money and create a folder for this year and one for last year.
6. Open 01 Clients and create a folder for each current client.

To add a file, open the folder where it belongs, click New, and choose File upload. On a phone, the Drive app has a plus button that lets you upload a file or scan a paper document with your camera. That scan feature is how paper receipts and letters get into your cabinet, and it means the shoebox can retire.

### The naming rule: Date - What - Who

Name every file the same way: *Date - What - Who*. For example:

- 2026-03 Invoice 004 - Johnson
- 2026-01 Business License - City of Baltimore
- 2025-12 Receipt Supplies - Home Depot
- 2026-04 Signed Agreement - Reed Law Office

Put the year in front, then the month as two digits. Written that way, your files line up in time order on their own. The "What" tells you the kind of document. The "Who" tells you which client, vendor, or agency it involves. With all three in the name, you never need to open a file to know what it is, and typing a few words in the search bar at the top of Drive brings it up in seconds.

Compare that with what files are usually called: "IMG_4471," "scan0003," or "invoice final FINAL 2." Those names tell you nothing. To rename a file, right-click it (or tap the three dots on a phone) and choose Rename.

### The habit that makes it work

The system only holds up if you feed it. The rule: a file arrives, it goes in Drive, named, that day. A customer emails a signed agreement? Save it to their client folder and rename it before you close the email. You buy supplies? Scan the receipt into this year's Money folder before you leave the parking lot. It takes about a minute each time, and it saves you hours later.

## Do this now

1. Open Drive from your business Gmail account and create your master folder with your business name.
2. Inside it, create the five drawers: 01 Clients, 02 Money, 03 Legal & Admin, 04 Marketing, 05 Operations.
3. Add year folders inside 02 Money and a folder for at least one client inside 01 Clients.
4. Gather five real business files you already have, on your phone, in your email, or on paper. Scan the paper ones with the Drive app.
5. Upload each one into the right drawer.
6. Rename each one using Date - What - Who.
7. Time it: close everything, then see how long it takes to pull up one of those files. Aim for ten seconds.

When you finish, you have a complete folder structure with five real files in it, named correctly and findable in seconds.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Uploading now and planning to organize later.** The pile grows and the sorting never happens. Fix: build the drawers, then upload straight into them.
- **Keeping business files in a personal Drive.** Mixed files are hard to hand to an accountant or lender. Fix: keep the master folder in your business account.
- **Leaving default names like "IMG_4471."** You end up opening every file to find the right one. Fix: rename with Date - What - Who the moment you upload.
- **Writing dates as "March 3" or "3-2-26."** Those do not sort in order. Fix: year, then month, like 2026-03.
- **Adding a drawer for every fresh idea.** Too many folders work no better than none. Fix: stick with the five and add sub-folders inside them only when one gets crowded.
