# Build Your Drive Filing System

> One master business folder, five drawers, and a file-naming rule that sorts itself so you can find anything in ten seconds.

## Why this matters

Sooner or later, someone asks you for a document, and they want it today. A lender asks for last year's tax return. A customer says they never got an invoice. A grant application wants your business license by 5 p.m. If your files are scattered across your phone, your email, a laptop desktop, and a shoebox, that request turns into a lost afternoon. A childcare provider with her license, insurance, and inspection report in one organized place can apply for a grant in the time it takes someone else to find their paperwork. A filing system is what lets you say yes quickly.

## What you'll learn

- What Google Drive is and why it beats saving files on one device
- How to build one master business folder with five numbered drawers
- How to name every file so it sorts itself
- The daily habit that keeps the system working

## The lesson

### Drive is your filing cabinet

Google Drive is online storage that comes with your Gmail account. Files you put in Drive live in your Google account, not on one device. That means you can open them from your phone, your laptop, or a library computer by signing in. If your phone breaks or your laptop is stolen, your files are still there. You can reach Drive from the grid of Google apps in the corner of Gmail, or through the Drive app on your phone.

Think of Drive as a filing cabinet. A cabinet only works if it has labeled drawers, and the rule is this: build the drawers before you upload. If you dump fifty files in first and plan to sort them later, later never comes.

### One master folder, five drawers

Start with one master folder named after your business. Everything about the business goes inside it, and nothing else does. Inside the master folder, create five folders:

```
Johnson Cleaning Co
  01 Clients
      Johnson, Mary
      Reed Law Office
  02 Money
      2025
      2026
  03 Legal & Admin
  04 Marketing
  05 Operations
```

The numbers in front keep the drawers in the same order every time, so your eyes always know where to look. Here is what goes in each one.

- **01 Clients.** One folder per client. Their quotes, agreements, photos of the job, and anything they send you.
- **02 Money.** Invoices, receipts, bank statements, and taxes, with a folder for each year. At tax time, you hand over one year's folder.
- **03 Legal & Admin.** Your business license, registration papers, tax ID letter, insurance, permits, and contracts with vendors.
- **04 Marketing.** Your logo, photos, flyers, social media graphics, and your business overview.
- **05 Operations.** How you run the work: price lists, checklists, schedules, supplier lists, and templates.

If you are not sure where a file goes, ask who or what it is about. About one customer? Clients. About dollars in or out? Money. Proof the business is legal? Legal & Admin.

### Building the folders

1. Open Drive and click the New button.
2. Choose New folder, type your business name, and create it.
3. Double-click the master folder to open it.
4. Click New, then New folder again, and create "01 Clients." Repeat for the other four.
5. Open 02 Money and create a folder for this year and one for last year.
6. Open 01 Clients and create a folder for each current client.

To add a file, open the folder where it belongs, click New, and choose File upload. On a phone, the Drive app has a plus button that lets you upload a file or scan a paper document with your camera. That scan feature is how paper receipts and letters get into your cabinet.

### The naming rule: Date — What — Who

Name every file the same way: *Date — What — Who*. For example:

- 2026-03 Invoice 004 — Johnson
- 2026-01 Business License — City of Baltimore
- 2025-12 Receipt Supplies — Home Depot
- 2026-04 Signed Agreement — Reed Law Office

Put the year first, then the month as two digits. When the date is written that way, your files line up in time order on their own. The "What" tells you the kind of document. The "Who" tells you which client, vendor, or agency it involves. With all three in the name, you never need to open a file to know what it is, and typing a few words in the search bar at the top of Drive finds it in seconds.

Compare that with what files are usually called: "IMG_4471," "scan0003," or "invoice final FINAL 2." Those names tell you nothing. To rename a file, right-click it (or tap the three dots on a phone) and choose Rename.

### The habit that makes it work

The system only holds up if you feed it. The rule: a new file arrives, it goes in Drive, named, that day. A customer emails a signed agreement? Save it to their client folder and rename it before you close the email. You buy supplies? Scan the receipt into this year's Money folder before you leave the parking lot. It takes about a minute each time, and it saves you hours later.

## Do this now

1. Open Drive from your business Gmail account and create your master folder with your business name.
2. Inside it, create the five drawers: 01 Clients, 02 Money, 03 Legal & Admin, 04 Marketing, 05 Operations.
3. Add year folders inside 02 Money and a folder for at least one client inside 01 Clients.
4. Find five real business files you already have, on your phone, in your email, or on paper. Scan the paper ones with the Drive app.
5. Upload each one into the right drawer.
6. Rename each one using Date — What — Who.
7. Test yourself: close everything, then time how long it takes to find one of those files. Aim for ten seconds.

When you finish, you have a complete folder structure with five real files in it, named correctly and findable in seconds.

## Common mistakes

- **Uploading first and planning to organize later.** The pile grows and the sorting never happens. Fix: build the drawers first, then upload straight into them.
- **Keeping business files in your personal Drive.** Mixed files are hard to hand to an accountant or lender. Fix: keep the master folder in your business account.
- **Leaving default names like "IMG_4471."** You will have to open every file to find the right one. Fix: rename with Date — What — Who the moment you upload.
- **Writing dates as "March 3" or "3-2-26."** Those do not sort in order. Fix: year first, then month, like 2026-03.
- **Adding extra drawers for every new idea.** Too many folders is the same as none. Fix: stick with the five and add sub-folders inside them only when one gets crowded.

## Check yourself

- What are the five drawers, in order?
- Why does the date go first in a file name, with the year before the month?
- Where would you file a photo of your business license? A receipt for gas? A flyer?
- A customer emails you a signed contract right now. What do you do with it, and when?

## What you walk away with

A master business folder in Drive with five drawers, a naming rule that sorts itself, and the daily habit that lets you find any business document in ten seconds.
