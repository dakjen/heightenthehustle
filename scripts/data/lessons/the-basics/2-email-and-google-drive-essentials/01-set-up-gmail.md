# Set Up Your Business Gmail

> Create a dedicated business Gmail with a professional address, 2-step verification, recovery info, and a signature.

## Why this matters

Your email address is often the first thing a customer, lender, or city office sees from you. An address like "sweetcheeks1987" tells them one story. An address like "johnsoncleaningco" tells them another. A dedicated business email also keeps your business from getting buried under family photos, store coupons, and school notices. Picture a caterer who misses a request for a 200-person wedding quote because it landed between a shoe sale and a group chat. A separate business inbox means every message in it is about your business, and nothing important slips past you.

## What you'll learn

- How to choose a professional email address that matches your business name
- How to create a Gmail account that is only for your business
- How to turn on 2-step verification and add recovery info so you never get locked out
- How to set a signature so every email carries your contact info automatically

## The lesson

### One inbox for the business, one for your life

A dedicated business email is an email account you use only for business. Customers, vendors, your bank, your licenses, your applications, and your online business accounts all go there. Your personal email stays personal. This split does two things for you. It makes you look established, and it makes your records easy to find later, because everything about the business lives in one place.

Gmail is free, it works on any phone or computer, and it comes with Google Drive, Docs, and Calendar. You will use all of those in this program, so one Gmail account opens the door to the rest.

### Choosing the address

Your address should be professional and close to your business name. No nicknames. No numbers. The test is simple: could you say it out loud over the phone once and have the other person type it correctly?

Good examples:

- johnsoncleaningco
- freshcutsbarbershop
- mariascateringbmore

Addresses to avoid:

- bigmike4life (a nickname)
- cleaning88421 (random numbers nobody will remember)
- jjj.cleaning.services.llc.md (too long and too easy to mistype)

If your exact business name is taken, do not reach for numbers. Add a short, meaningful word instead, like your city, "co," "hello," or "info." For example, "hellofreshcuts" or "freshcutsbaltimore." Write down two or three options before you start, because your first choice may already belong to someone else.

### Creating the account

1. Go to the Gmail sign-in page and choose the option to create an account. If Google asks what the account is for, either choice works for a free Gmail address.
2. For the name, enter your business name, or your own name if you are the face of the business. This is the name people see in their inbox when you write to them.
3. Type the address you chose. If it is taken, try your next option.
4. Create a strong password that you do not use anywhere else. A long phrase of several unrelated words is easier to remember and harder to guess than a short, tricky password. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe at home, not on a sticky note on your screen.
5. Finish the steps Google gives you and sign in.

### Locking the door: 2-step verification and recovery info

2-step verification means that signing in takes two things: your password, and a second proof that it is really you, usually a code or a prompt sent to your phone. If someone steals or guesses your password, they still cannot get in without your phone. For a business, this matters a lot. Your email is the key to almost everything else, because "forgot my password" links for your bank, your payment app, and your social media all go to your inbox.

To turn it on, open your Google Account settings (click your profile circle in the top corner, then "Manage your Google Account"), find the Security section, and look for 2-Step Verification. Follow the prompts to connect your phone.

Recovery info is a phone number and a second email address that Google can use to get you back in if you forget your password or get locked out. You will find it in the same Security section. Add both. Use your personal email as the recovery email. Check this info once a year, and update it the same day you ever change your phone number.

### A signature that does the work for you

A signature is a short block of text that Gmail adds to the bottom of every email you send. Set it once and every message carries your contact info without you typing it. Keep it to three or four lines:

```
Tanya Johnson
Johnson Cleaning Co.
(410) 555-0142
```

To set it, open Settings (the gear icon), choose to see all settings, and scroll through the General section until you find Signature. Create a new signature, type your lines, choose it as the default for new emails and for replies, and save your changes at the bottom of the page.

## Do this now

1. Write down three possible addresses that are close to your business name, with no nicknames and no numbers.
2. Create your business Gmail account using the first one that is available.
3. Open your Google Account settings, go to Security, and turn on 2-Step Verification with your phone.
4. In the same Security section, add your recovery phone number and your personal email as the recovery email.
5. Open Gmail Settings, find Signature, and add your name, business name, and phone number. Set it as the default and save.
6. Send an email from your new account to your personal email. Open it and confirm that the sender name looks right and the signature shows up.

When you finish, you have a working, secured business email, and a test message in your personal inbox that proves it.

## Common mistakes

- **Using your personal email "for now."** "For now" turns into years, and your business records end up mixed with everything else. Fix: create the business account today and give out only that address from here on.
- **Adding numbers because the name was taken.** Numbers are hard to remember and look less professional. Fix: add your city, "co," "hello," or "info" instead.
- **Skipping 2-step verification because it feels like a hassle.** One stolen password can cost you your email and every account tied to it. Fix: turn it on now. It adds a few seconds when you sign in on a new device.
- **Leaving recovery info blank or out of date.** If you get locked out with no recovery info, getting back in can be very hard. Fix: add a phone and a second email, and update them whenever they change.
- **A signature that is a whole paragraph.** Long quotes and five phone numbers get ignored. Fix: name, business, phone. One more line at most.

## Check yourself

- Could you say your new address over the phone once and have someone type it correctly?
- If someone learned your password today, what would stop them from signing in?
- Where would Google send a recovery code if you were locked out, and is that information current?
- What three things appear at the bottom of every email you send?

## What you walk away with

A dedicated business Gmail with a professional address, protected by 2-step verification and recovery info, with a signature that puts your name, business, and phone number on every email you send.
