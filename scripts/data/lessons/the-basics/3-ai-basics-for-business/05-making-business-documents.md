# Making Business Documents

> Template for the bones, AI for the first draft, you for the facts and the voice, PDF out the door.

## Why this matters

At some point every business gets asked for something on paper. A property manager wants a letter. A church wants a flyer for its bulletin board. A bank officer asks, "Do you have something that explains what you do?" The owner who can hand over a clean, professional document on the spot looks ready for bigger work. The owner who says "let me get back to you" often does not get a second chance. A handyman with a sharp one-page overview and clear service descriptions can walk into a property management office and leave it behind. You do not need a designer for any of this. You need a template, an AI tool, and about an hour.

## What you'll learn

- The formatting basics that make any document look professional
- Why you start from a template and always send a PDF
- How to build four core documents: a letter, a flyer, a one-page overview, and service descriptions
- The four-step pattern that works for every document

## The lesson

### The formatting basics apply to everything

Every business needs a handful of professional documents, and AI plus Google Docs or Word gets you all of them. Four formatting rules cover every one.

**Real headings, not just bold text.** In Docs and Word, a heading is a style you pick from the styles menu in the toolbar (it usually shows "Normal text" or "Normal"). Choosing "Heading 1" or "Heading 2" keeps every heading the same size and look, and it lets the program build an outline of your document. Making text bold and bigger by hand looks uneven and is harder to fix later.

**One clean font.** Pick one easy-to-read font and use it for the whole document. Mixing three or four fonts is the fastest way to look unprofessional.

**Consistent spacing.** Use the same space between paragraphs and the same margins on every page. If one section is cramped and another is airy, readers notice.

**Always send as PDF.** A PDF looks the same on every device, and a Doc file doesn't. A Word or Docs file can shift its fonts and layout when opened on a different phone or computer, and the other person can change it. In Google Docs, open the File menu, choose Download, and pick PDF. In Word, use Save As or Export and choose PDF.

### Start from a template

Start from an HTH template instead of a blank page whenever one exists. You will find them in this lesson's downloads. A template already has the layout, headings, font, and spacing set up. AI fills in the words and the template handles the look. When you open a template, make your own copy first so the original stays clean, then put your copy in the right folder in your Drive.

### The documents to build

**A business letter on your letterhead.** Letterhead is the strip at the top of the page with your business name, address, phone, and email. Tell AI who the letter is to, what it's about, and the tone. It drafts, you fix.

> I own a commercial cleaning company. Write a one-page business letter to a property manager introducing my company and asking for a 15-minute meeting about cleaning services for their office building. Professional and warm. Leave placeholders for names and dates.

A good draft has a short opening that says why you are writing, one paragraph on what you offer, and a clear request for the meeting.

**A flyer.** AI writes the headline and the short, punchy lines. You drop them into a template with your photo and contact info.

> Write flyer text for a mobile car detailing business offering a $20-off spring special. Give me 3 headline options under 8 words, 3 short bullet points, and one line telling people how to book.

A good answer is brief. A flyer is read in about three seconds, so if AI gives you paragraphs, tell it to cut.

**A one-page business overview.** This is the leave-behind with who you are, what you offer, and how to reach you. AI structures it from your rambling description. Type or speak everything about your business in whatever order it comes out, then add:

> Organize this into a one-page business overview with these sections: Who We Are, What We Offer, Who We Serve, and How to Reach Us. Keep it short and plain. Do not add any facts I did not give you.

**Service descriptions.** Two or three sentences per service, clear enough that a stranger knows what they're buying. Paste your service list into AI and ask for plain-English descriptions, then make them true.

> Here is my service list: standard clean, deep clean, move-out clean. Write a 2 to 3 sentence plain-English description of each for a homeowner who has never hired a cleaner.

"Make them true" is the key step. AI will guess what a deep clean includes. If its draft says you clean inside the oven and you do not, a customer will expect it. Fix every detail to match what you really do.

### The pattern every time

1. **Template for the bones.** Open the template and make your copy.
2. **AI for the first draft.** Give it context, a specific task, and a format. Keep private information out.
3. **You for the facts and the voice.** Correct every name, price, date, and promise. Rewrite until it sounds like you.
4. **PDF out the door.** Download as PDF, name it with Date — What — Who, and save it in your Drive.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting from a blank page.** You spend an hour fighting layout before you write a word. Fix: start from a template whenever one exists.
- **Sending the editable Doc or Word file.** It can look broken on the other person's device and can be changed. Fix: always send a PDF.
- **Making headings with bold and a bigger font size.** They end up uneven. Fix: use the heading styles in the toolbar.
- **Leaving AI's guesses in your service descriptions.** Customers will hold you to what is written. Fix: correct every detail to match what you deliver.
- **Cramming a flyer with text.** Nobody reads a crowded flyer. Fix: one headline, three short points, one way to reach you.
