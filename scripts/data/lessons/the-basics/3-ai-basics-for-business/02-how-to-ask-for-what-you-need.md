# Write Prompts That Get Usable Drafts

> Give AI context, a specific task and a format, then refine the draft until it sounds like you.

## Why this matters

The quality of what AI gives you depends on what you give it. Type "write an ad" and you get something bland that could be for any business in any city. Tell it who you are, what you want and how you want it, and you get something you can use in a few minutes. That is the difference between a tool that wastes your time and one that saves it. A cleaning business owner who asks well can turn out a week of client texts, a reply to a tough review and a service description in one sitting.

## What you'll be able to do

- Brief AI the way you would brief a smart employee on day one
- Build every request from three parts: context, task and format
- Refine a draft with follow-up messages without starting over
- Edit AI writing until it sounds like you, with every fact checked
- Save the prompts that work so repeat jobs take seconds

## The lesson

### Brief it like a smart employee on day one

What you type into an AI tool is called a prompt. The most reliable way to write one is to picture a smart employee on day one. They are capable and willing, and they know nothing about your business. Tell that person "write something for our customers" and they have to guess at everything. Tell them who your customers are, what the message is for and how long it should be, and they do well. AI works the same way. It cannot read your mind, and it fills every gap you leave with a guess.

### Three parts in every request

Every strong prompt has three parts.

**Context.** Who you are and who this is for. "I run a cleaning business in Baltimore."

**A specific task.** Exactly what you want made. "Write a short, friendly text offering past clients 15% off a spring deep-clean."

**A format.** The shape of the answer: how long, how many versions, what style. "Under 50 words, three options."

Put together, the prompt reads:

> I run a cleaning business in Baltimore. Write a short, friendly text offering past clients 15% off a spring deep-clean. Under 50 words, three options.

Compare that to the weak version: "Write a text about a sale." The weak prompt gets you a message with a made-up discount, no mention of cleaning, and a pile of exclamation points. The strong prompt gets you three short options that name your real offer, something like: "Hi! It's Tanya from Johnson Cleaning. Spring is here, and past clients get 15% off a deep-clean this month. Want me to save you a spot?" A good answer uses your details, stays inside your word limit, and sounds like something a real person would send.

### The same three parts in other trades

> I own a two-chair barbershop. A customer left a two-star review saying he waited 40 minutes past his appointment time. Write a calm, professional public reply that apologizes, does not make excuses, and invites him back. Under 80 words.

> I run a home daycare for children ages 1 to 4. Write a welcome note for new parents that explains drop-off time is 7:30 to 8:30 a.m. and asks them to pack a change of clothes. Warm tone, one short paragraph.

The real facts, like the wait time and the drop-off hours, come from you. AI supplies the wording. If you leave a fact out, it may invent one.

### Refine in the same chat

Treat the answer as a draft. You are in a conversation, and the tool remembers what was said earlier in the same chat. So do not start over. Reply, refine, redirect. The back-and-forth costs nothing. Useful follow-ups include:

- "Shorter. Cut it in half."
- "Too formal. Make it sound like a text from a neighbor."
- "I like option 2. Give me three more like that one."
- "Take out the exclamation points and the word 'thrilled.'"
- "Add that the offer ends April 30."

Three or four rounds is normal. Each round takes seconds, and each one moves the draft closer to what you want.

### Make it sound like you

AI writing has habits. It tends to be a little too polished, a little too excited, and fond of words you would never say out loud. Your customers know your voice, and a message that does not sound like you can read as cold or fake. So the last step is always yours.

- Read the draft out loud.
- Change any word you would not use.
- Check every fact, name, price and date.
- Add the personal touch only you know, like asking how the new puppy is doing.

You can also get closer on the first try by pasting in a message you wrote yourself and saying, "Match the tone of this."

### Save what works

Owners who get the most from these tools stop retyping. Two things are worth setting up once.

- **A context line.** Keep one or two sentences about your business in your phone's notes: what you do, where, and who you serve. Paste it at the top of every prompt.
- **A prompt file.** When a prompt produces a draft you sent with little editing, save it. Your appointment reminder, review reply and special-offer prompts will be reused every month with only the details changed.

## Do this now

1. Pick one real client you have a reason to text this week, such as a reminder, a thank-you, a special offer or a check-in.
2. Open your AI tool and write a prompt with all three parts: context about your business, the specific task, and the format you want.
3. Leave out the client's full name, phone number, address and any private details. Use a first name or "a past client."
4. Read the answer and send at least two follow-up messages to refine it.
5. Copy the best version and edit it by hand until it sounds like you. Read it out loud and check the facts.
6. Send the text to your client.

When you finish, you have sent a real message to a real client, drafted with AI and finished in your own voice.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **One-line prompts with no details.** Vague in, vague out, and the rewrite takes longer than writing it yourself. Fix: include context, task and format every time.
- **Opening a fresh chat when the answer misses.** Everything the tool already knows about the job is gone. Fix: reply in the same chat and say what to change.
- **Sending the draft as is.** It reads like a machine wrote it and may carry wrong details to a customer. Fix: refine, then edit by hand and read it out loud.
- **Leaving out the real facts.** The tool fills the gap with invented prices, dates or offers that you may end up honoring. Fix: give it the real numbers and check them in the result.
- **Not asking for a length.** You get five paragraphs when you needed two sentences. Fix: say "under 50 words" or "one short paragraph."

## Before you move on

- Did the message you sent this week contain all three parts in its prompt: context, task and format?
- Can you paste your business context line into a prompt in under ten seconds?
- Which two follow-up messages moved your draft the most, and will you reuse them?
- Did you read the final version out loud and confirm every name, price and date before sending?
- Which prompt from today is worth saving for next month?

## What you walk away with

A working formula for every request, context plus task plus format, the habit of refining in the same chat, and a real client message sent in your own voice.
