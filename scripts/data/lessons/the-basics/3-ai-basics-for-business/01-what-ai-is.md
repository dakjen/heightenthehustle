# What AI Is

> AI is a fast, eager assistant whose work you review, not a source of truth. Learn what it does well and where it goes wrong.

## Why this matters

When you run a small business, you are the owner, the marketer, the bookkeeper, and the customer service desk. Most of that work involves words: texts to customers, replies to complaints, service descriptions, social media posts, and forms. AI tools can take the first pass at that writing in seconds. A barber who dreads writing can describe a new loyalty deal in one messy sentence and get back three clean versions to choose from. It will not run your business for you, and it will make mistakes. Used with open eyes, though, it gives a one-person business some of the help a bigger company pays staff for.

## What you'll learn

- What tools like ChatGPT and Claude are, in plain language
- What AI is good at and what it is bad at
- Why AI can be confidently wrong, and what that means for how you use it
- How to get started with a free account

## The lesson

### What these tools are

When people say "AI" today, they usually mean chat tools like ChatGPT and Claude. You type a question or a request in plain English, and the tool types back an answer. There is nothing to install and no special commands to learn. If you can send a text message, you can use it.

Here is what is happening behind the screen. These tools are computer programs that learned the patterns in human writing by processing a huge amount of text. Because of that, they are very good at producing writing that fits whatever you ask for. Ask for a polite reminder about an overdue invoice and you get one, because the tool has learned what polite reminders look like.

### What AI is good at

AI is excellent at anything involving words. Four jobs stand out:

- **Drafting.** Writing a first version of an email, a text, a post, or a description.
- **Rewriting.** Taking something you wrote and making it shorter, friendlier, more professional, or clearer.
- **Summarizing.** Turning a long document, like a lease or a grant notice, into the main points.
- **Brainstorming.** Giving you ten name ideas, twenty post topics, or a list of questions to ask a landlord.

For example, you could type: "I own a mobile car detailing business. Give me five ideas for a slow-season special." In a few seconds you would have five ideas. Two might be useless. One might be something you never thought of. That is a good trade for ten seconds of your time.

### Where AI goes wrong

AI can be confidently wrong. This is the most important thing to understand about it. The tool is built to produce writing that sounds right. It does not check its answers against the real world the way you would check a fact. So it can give you a wrong deadline, a law that does not exist, a made-up statistic, or a phone number that goes nowhere, and say it in the same calm, sure voice it uses when it is correct. Nothing in the answer will warn you.

It also does not know your business. It does not know your prices, your customers, your city's rules, or what happened last Tuesday unless you tell it. Some tools can search the internet and some cannot, and even the ones that can still make mistakes. Assume its knowledge may be out of date or incomplete.

### The right way to think about it

Treat AI like a fast, eager assistant whose work you review, not a source of truth. Picture a new helper who is quick, never tired, happy to redo anything, and sometimes makes things up without knowing it. You would give that helper plenty of work. You would also read everything before it went out the door. That is the relationship.

In practice, this means two things. For words and ideas, lean on it heavily. For facts that matter, like tax rules, license requirements, legal questions, prices, and deadlines, check a real source before you rely on the answer. Later lessons in this unit cover how to ask well and how to stay safe.

### Getting started

Free versions are plenty to start. The well-known tools offer a free level that handles everyday writing and questions. What each free level includes changes over time, so do not worry about picking the perfect one. Pick one, and you can try another later.

1. Choose one tool, such as ChatGPT or Claude.
2. Open its website or download its app from your phone's official app store. Check that the app is from the real company, because copycat apps exist.
3. Create a free account. Using your business Gmail keeps things in one place.
4. Find the message box, usually at the bottom of the screen. Type your question and send it.

## Do this now

1. Create a free account on one AI tool using your business email.
2. Think of one real question about your business. Not a test question. Something you have been wondering about, like "What should I include in a quote for a house cleaning job?" or "What are some ways a small barbershop can bring back customers who stopped coming?"
3. Type it in and include one sentence about your business, such as what you do and where.
4. Read the whole answer. Mark what is useful, what is too general, and anything you are not sure is true.
5. Type one follow-up, such as "Make that shorter" or "Give me more detail on the second idea."

When you finish, you have a working account and a first real answer about your own business, along with your own notes on what was useful and what needs checking.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing everything because it sounds confident.** A sure tone is not proof. Fix: check any fact that matters against a real source before you act on it.
- **Giving up after one weak answer.** A general question gets a general answer. Fix: add details about your business and ask again.
- **Thinking you need a paid plan to begin.** Free versions are plenty to start. Fix: use the free level until you hit a limit you care about.
- **Using it like a search engine only.** Looking things up is its weakest skill. Fix: use it for drafting, rewriting, summarizing, and brainstorming, where it is strongest.
- **Downloading a look-alike app.** Fake apps copy the names of real tools. Fix: confirm the company name in the app store before you install.
