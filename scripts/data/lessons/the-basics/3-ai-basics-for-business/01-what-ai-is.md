# Where AI Saves You Time and Where It Costs You

> Hand AI the drafting, rewriting, summarizing and brainstorming, and check every fact that matters before it leaves your hands.

## Why this matters

You are the owner, the marketer, the bookkeeper and the customer service desk. Most of that work is words: texts to customers, replies to complaints, service descriptions, social posts and forms. AI chat tools such as ChatGPT and Claude take the first pass at that writing in seconds. A barber with a full chair all day can describe a new loyalty deal in one rough sentence between cuts and get back three clean versions to choose from. The tool will not run the business, and it makes mistakes that look exactly like correct answers. Owners who know which jobs to hand it, and which answers to check, get some of the help a bigger company pays staff for.

## What you'll be able to do

- Hand AI the four jobs it does well and keep it away from the ones it does badly
- Spot the kind of answer that needs checking before you act on it
- Set up a free account on your business email and avoid copycat apps
- Get a useful answer to a real question about your own business, then sharpen it with a follow-up

## The lesson

### Four jobs worth handing over

These tools learned the patterns of human writing from an enormous amount of text. That makes them strong at producing writing that fits a request. Ask for a polite reminder about an overdue invoice and you get one, because the tool has seen what polite reminders look like. Four jobs stand out.

- **Drafting.** A first version of an email, a text, a post or a description.
- **Rewriting.** Taking something you wrote and making it shorter, friendlier, more professional or clearer.
- **Summarizing.** Turning a long document, like a lease or a grant notice, into the main points.
- **Brainstorming.** Ten name ideas, twenty post topics, or a list of questions to ask a landlord.

For example, type: "I own a mobile car detailing business. Give me five ideas for a slow-season special." In a few seconds you have five ideas. Two might be useless. One might be something you had not considered. That is a good trade for ten seconds of your time.

### Where it costs you: confident wrong answers

The tool is built to produce writing that sounds right. It does not check its answers against the real world the way you would check a fact. So it can give you a wrong deadline, a law that does not exist, a made-up statistic, or a phone number that goes nowhere, in the same calm, sure voice it uses when it is correct. Nothing in the answer flags the difference.

It also does not know your business. It does not know your prices, your customers, your city's rules, or what happened last Tuesday unless you tell it. Some tools can search the internet and some cannot, and the ones that can still make mistakes. Assume its knowledge may be out of date or incomplete.

### The working relationship

Treat AI as a fast, eager assistant whose work you review, not a source of truth. Think of a hire who is quick, never tired, happy to redo anything, and who sometimes makes things up without knowing it. You would give that person plenty of work. You would also read everything before it went out the door.

In practice that splits into two rules.

- For words and ideas, lean on it heavily.
- For facts that matter, such as tax rules, license requirements, legal questions, prices and deadlines, check a real source before you rely on the answer.

### Set up the account

The free level of the well-known tools handles everyday writing and questions. What each free level includes changes over time, so the choice of tool matters less than putting one to work. You can switch later without losing anything important.

1. Choose one tool, such as ChatGPT or Claude.
2. Open its website, or download its app from your phone's official app store. Confirm the developer is the real company, because copycat apps use near-identical names.
3. Create a free account with your business Gmail so the account stays with the business.
4. Type your request in the message box and send it.

### Get more from every request

Three habits separate owners who save real time from owners who get generic answers.

- **Open with one line about the business.** "I run a two-chair barbershop in a college neighborhood" changes every answer that follows.
- **Stay in the same chat and follow up.** "Make that shorter" or "More detail on the second idea" takes seconds and uses everything the tool already knows.
- **Dictate when you are on the move.** The microphone on your phone keyboard lets you talk a request in between jobs. A rough spoken request still produces a clean draft.

## Do this now

1. Create a free account on one AI tool using your business email.
2. Choose one real question about your business. Not a test question. Something you have been weighing, like "What should I include in a quote for a house cleaning job?" or "What are some ways a small barbershop can bring back customers who stopped coming?"
3. Type it in and include one sentence about your business, such as what you do and where.
4. Read the whole answer. Mark what is useful, what is too general, and anything you are not sure is true.
5. Send one follow-up, such as "Make that shorter" or "Give me more detail on the second idea."

When you finish, you have a working account and a real answer about your own business, along with your own notes on what was useful and what needs checking.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Acting on an answer because it sounds sure.** A confident tone is not proof, and a wrong deadline or license rule is expensive. Fix: check any fact that matters against a real source before you act on it.
- **Dropping the tool after one generic answer.** A general question gets a general answer. Fix: add details about your business and ask again in the same chat.
- **Paying for a plan before you need one.** The free level covers everyday writing. Fix: stay on it until you hit a limit you care about.
- **Using it mainly to look things up.** Fact lookup is its weakest skill. Fix: use it for drafting, rewriting, summarizing and brainstorming, where it is strongest.
- **Installing a look-alike app.** Copycat apps borrow the names of real tools and can charge you or collect what you type. Fix: confirm the company name in the app store before you install.

## Before you move on

- Is your AI account on your business email, and is the app from the real company?
- Which piece of writing on this week's list will you hand to AI as a draft?
- Which answer from today's session needs checking, and where will you check it?
- Can you name the facts in your business, such as license rules, tax dates and prices, that always get verified at the source?
- Did your follow-up message improve the answer, and what detail made the difference?

## What you walk away with

A free AI account on your business email, a real answer about your own business, and a working rule for every future use: AI drafts fast, and you review everything before it goes out.
