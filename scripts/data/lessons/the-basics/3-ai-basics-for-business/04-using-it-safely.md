# Keep Customer Data and Your Name Protected When You Use AI

> Four rules that protect your customers, your money and your reputation every time you use an AI tool.

## Why this matters

Your business runs on trust. Customers give you their addresses, their phone numbers, sometimes the keys to their homes. Funders and agencies take what you write in an application as true. AI tools create two specific ways to damage that trust, and both happen to capable people working fast. A home health aide finishing care notes at the end of a long shift pastes a client's full name, address and medical needs into a chat tool to speed up the write-up. A contractor on a bid deadline copies an AI answer about permit rules into the bid, and the rule turns out to be wrong. Neither meant harm. Both now have a problem. Four rules prevent almost all of it.

## What you'll be able to do

- Keep customer, banking and login information out of AI tools and still get the draft you need
- Verify any answer that affects your money, legal standing or customers at the right source
- Catch invented details and leftover placeholders before a message goes out under your name
- Keep unchecked AI "facts" out of applications and client documents

## The lesson

### Rule 1: Never paste in what you wouldn't put on a postcard

A postcard can be read by anyone who handles it. Treat an AI chat the same way. What you type is sent to the company that runs the tool and stored on its computers. Depending on the tool and your settings, it may be kept, reviewed, or used to improve the product. Policies differ and they change, so the reliable habit is to keep private information out to begin with.

That means:

- **No customer data.** No full names with addresses, phone numbers, emails, health details or payment information.
- **No banking info.** No account numbers, routing numbers, card numbers, or tax ID and Social Security numbers.
- **No passwords.** Not yours, not anyone's, not ever.

You still get the help you need. Swap the private details for placeholders. Here is the unsafe version of a prompt:

> Write a late payment reminder to Denise Carter at 4410 Elm Ave, 410-555-0199, who owes $380 on card ending 4421.

Here is the safe version:

> Write a polite late payment reminder to a client named [NAME] who owes [AMOUNT] for a cleaning service from three weeks ago. Friendly but firm. Under 80 words.

The draft is the same quality. Then you fill in the real name and amount yourself, outside the AI tool.

### Rule 2: Verify anything that matters against a real source

AI can be confidently wrong. It can state a wrong tax deadline, a license rule from a different state, or a statistic it made up, and it sounds as sure as when it is right. So any answer that affects your money, your legal standing or your customers gets checked against a real source before you act on it.

A real source is the place the information comes from.

- For a license rule, the city or state agency that issues the license.
- For a tax question, the tax agency or your accountant.
- For a contract question, a lawyer.
- For a product, the manufacturer's label or instructions.

AI is not your lawyer or accountant. It can help you get up to speed on a topic and prepare sharp questions, and then a real person or official source gives you the answer you rely on.

If the tool shows you a source for its claim, go look at that source yourself. Sometimes the source does not say what the tool claimed, and sometimes it does not exist.

### Rule 3: Never send AI-written text you haven't read

Whatever you send goes out under your name. The customer does not know or care that a tool drafted it. If the message promises a discount you do not offer, gives the wrong date, or sounds cold, that is you saying it. AI drafts can include invented details, placeholder text like "[insert date]," or a promise you never made.

Before anything leaves your hands, read every word. Check names, dates, prices and promises. Read it out loud once to catch anything that does not sound like you. This takes a minute or two, and it protects years of reputation.

### Rule 4: Never pass along unchecked AI "facts" in applications or client documents

Applications and client documents are held to a higher standard, because other people make decisions based on them. A grant reviewer, a lender, or a customer signing your agreement assumes what is written is true. If an AI tool adds a number to your grant application, such as how many businesses like yours exist in your city, and that number is invented, you have submitted false information, even if you did not know it.

So for these documents, every fact must be one you can back up. Your own numbers, like your revenue, your years in business and your customer count, come from your own records. Any outside fact gets verified, and you keep a note of where it came from. If you cannot verify it, take it out. AI can help with the wording. The facts have to be yours.

### The four-question check before you hit send

1. Did I keep private information out of the tool?
2. Did I verify the facts that matter?
3. Did I read every word?
4. Could I back up every claim if someone asked?

If staff or family help with your messages and paperwork, give them the same four questions.

## Do this now

1. Choose a factual question about your industry where the answer matters. Examples: "What license does a home daycare need in my state?" or "How long should cooked food be kept hot at a catered event?"
2. Ask your AI tool the question. Include your state or city if it applies.
3. Write down the answer it gives, including any specific numbers, names or rules.
4. Check it against a real source: the agency that makes the rule, an official publication, or a licensed professional you know.
5. Compare the two. Note what the AI got right, what it got wrong, and what it left out.
6. Write one sentence on what the comparison showed you.

When you finish, you have a side-by-side comparison of an AI answer and a real source, and firsthand evidence of why you verify.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Pasting a whole customer list or invoice into the chat.** That hands private data to a third party. Fix: remove or replace names, contact details and payment info before you paste anything.
- **Assuming a detailed answer must be correct.** Specific numbers and official-sounding names can be invented. Fix: confirm with the agency, the professional or the original document.
- **Copying and sending without reading.** Wrong details and leftover placeholders go out under your name. Fix: read every word, out loud if you can.
- **Letting AI add statistics to a grant or loan application.** You are responsible for every claim. Fix: use only facts you can trace to your records or a real source.
- **Using AI in place of a lawyer or accountant.** It can explain, but it cannot take responsibility. Fix: use it to prepare your questions, then ask the professional.

## Before you move on

- Look at your last few AI chats. Is there a customer name, address, account number or password in any of them?
- For the license, tax and contract questions in your trade, do you know which agency or professional is the real source?
- Does every outside fact in your current grant, loan or bid paperwork have a note saying where it came from?
- Does everyone who writes on behalf of your business know the four-question check?

## What you walk away with

Four rules to apply every time you use AI: keep private data out, verify what matters, read before you send, and keep unchecked facts out of anything official.
