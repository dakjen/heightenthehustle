# Sharing & Collaborating

> Stop emailing attachments back and forth. Keep one live file in Drive, share it with the right people, and give the lowest permission that works.

## Why this matters

When you email a file as an attachment, you create a copy. The other person edits their copy and sends it back, and now there are two. A week later there are five, named things like "proposal FINAL v3 revised," and nobody is sure which one is right. Think of Malik, a contractor sending a kitchen remodel proposal to a client. They trade six attachments, and the client signs an old version with the wrong cabinet price. That mistake costs real money. One shared file would have prevented it.

## What you'll learn

- Why one live file beats attachments
- The three permission levels and when to use each
- How to share with specific people
- How automatic saving and version history protect you
- Why you share the folder, not file by file

## The lesson

### One file, in Drive

Google Drive is your online filing cabinet. Files you make in Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides live there, and you can upload other files too. When you share a Drive file, you are not sending a copy. You are giving someone a key to the one and only file. Everyone with a key sees the same thing, and every change shows up for everyone right away.

That ends attachment ping-pong, the back-and-forth of copies. There is no "which version" question because there is only one version. The link you sent last month still opens the current file today.

### The three permission levels

When you share, you choose how much each person can do. Give the lowest permission that works. You can always raise it later, and it is much harder to undo an unwanted change.

| Level | What they can do | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer | Look only | A price list for customers, a finished contract for reference, a schedule staff should read but not change |
| Commenter | Look, leave comments, and suggest changes you approve or reject | A client reviewing a proposal, a mentor giving feedback on your plan |
| Editor | Change anything, and usually share it with others | A business partner, your bookkeeper, an employee who updates the file as part of the job |

The short way to remember it: Viewer looks, Commenter suggests, Editor changes.

The proposal case is worth a closer look. A client reviewing a proposal is a Commenter. They can highlight a line and write "Can we use the less expensive tile here?" You see the note, reply, and make the change yourself. If you had made the client an Editor, they could change your price or delete a paragraph, and you might not notice until after signing.

### How to share with specific people

1. Open the file and click the Share button.
2. Type the person's email address.
3. Choose Viewer, Commenter, or Editor from the menu beside their name.
4. Add a short note saying what you need, such as "Please comment by Thursday."
5. Send.

The share window also has a general access setting. "Restricted" means only the people you added can open the file. "Anyone with the link" means anyone who gets the link can open it, even if it is forwarded to a stranger. For business files, such as proposals, pricing, customer lists, and anything with personal information, keep it Restricted and share with specific people. Use "Anyone with the link" only for things that are meant to be public, such as a menu or a flyer, and set it to Viewer.

You can open the Share window at any time to see who has access, change someone's level, or remove them. Do this when a project ends or an employee leaves.

### Automatic saving and version history

Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides save on their own as you type. There is no Save button to forget, and a dead battery does not cost you your work.

Version history is the record of every earlier state of the file. You find it in the File menu. It shows who changed what and when, and it lets you restore an older version. If someone deletes a section by accident, you can bring it back. If a client says "that is not the price we agreed to," you can show what the file said on the day they approved it. Before an important moment, such as sending a proposal for signature, you can name the current version so it is easy to find later.

### Share the folder, not file by file

A folder in Drive can be shared the same way a file can. Everything inside it carries the same access, and so does anything you add later. So instead of sharing twelve files one at a time, you make one folder and share it once.

Malik sets up his work this way. Each client gets a folder named with their last name and the job, such as "Harris, kitchen remodel." Inside are the proposal, the signed contract, photos, and invoices. He shares that folder with the client as a Commenter or Viewer. His bookkeeper gets Editor access to a separate folder that holds receipts and invoices for all jobs. One caution follows from this: before you put a file in a shared folder, remember who can see that folder. Keep your private cost notes somewhere else.

## Do this now

1. In Drive, create a folder for one real client or project and name it clearly.
2. Create or move one document into it, such as a quote or a proposal.
3. Share the folder with a neighbor in the session, or with a second email address of your own, as a Commenter.
4. Have them leave one comment and one suggested change.
5. Reply to the comment and accept or reject the suggestion.
6. Open version history and find the version from before their change.
7. Open the Share window, check the general access setting, and then change your neighbor to Viewer.

Finished result: one shared client folder with a live document, a resolved comment, and permissions you chose on purpose.

## Common mistakes

- **Making everyone an Editor because it is easiest.** Fix: start with the lowest level that works and raise it only if needed.
- **Using "Anyone with the link" for private files.** Fix: keep business files Restricted and add people by email.
- **Downloading the file and emailing it anyway.** Fix: send the link, so there is still only one copy.
- **Sharing files one at a time.** Fix: build a folder per client or project and share that.
- **Never removing access.** Fix: review who has access when a job or a working relationship ends.

## Check yourself

- What is the difference between a Viewer, a Commenter, and an Editor?
- Which level does a client reviewing a proposal get, and why?
- Where would you look to recover something that was deleted from a document?
- Which of your files are set to "Anyone with the link" right now?

## What you walk away with

A client folder set up in Drive, a clear rule for choosing permissions, and the confidence that one live file with full history has replaced the pile of attachments.
