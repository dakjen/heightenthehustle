# One Live File: Share Drive Folders with the Right Permissions

> Stop emailing attachments back and forth. Keep one live file in Drive, share it with the right people, and give the lowest permission that works.

## Why this matters

Every attachment you email is a copy. The other person edits their copy and sends it back, and now there are two. A week later there are five, named things like "proposal FINAL v3 revised," and it takes real effort to tell which one is right. Think of Malik, a contractor sending a kitchen remodel proposal to a client. Over two weeks of changes they trade six attachments, and the client signs an earlier version with the wrong cabinet price. That gap comes out of Malik's margin or turns into a hard conversation. One shared file would have left only one version to sign.

## What you'll be able to do

- Replace attachments with one live file that everyone works from
- Choose among the three permission levels for each person
- Share with specific people and control who else can open the link
- Use version history to recover work and to prove what was agreed
- Share one folder per client in place of file-by-file sharing

## The lesson

### One file, in Drive

Files you make in Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides live in Google Drive, and you can upload other files there too. When you share a Drive file, you are not sending a copy. You are giving someone a key to the one and only file. Everyone with a key sees the same thing, and every change shows up for everyone right away.

That ends attachment ping-pong, the back-and-forth of copies. There is no "which version" question because there is only one version. The link you sent last month still opens the current file today.

### The three permission levels

When you share, you choose how much each person can do. Give the lowest permission that works. You can always raise it later, and it is much harder to undo an unwanted change.

| Level | What they can do | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Viewer | Look only | A price list for customers, a finished contract for reference, a schedule staff should read but not change |
| Commenter | Look, leave comments, and suggest changes you approve or reject | A client reviewing a proposal, a mentor giving feedback on your plan |
| Editor | Change anything, and usually share it with others | A business partner, your bookkeeper, an employee who updates the file as part of the job |

In short: Viewer looks, Commenter suggests, Editor changes.

The proposal case is worth a closer look. A client reviewing a proposal is a Commenter. They can highlight a line and write "Can we use the less expensive tile here?" You see the note, reply, and make the change yourself. As an Editor, the same client could change your price or delete a paragraph, and you might not notice until after signing.

### How to share with specific people

1. Open the file and click the Share button.
2. Type the person's email address.
3. Choose Viewer, Commenter, or Editor from the menu beside their name.
4. Add a short note saying what you need, such as "Please comment by Thursday."
5. Send.

The share window also has a general access setting. "Restricted" means only the people you added can open the file. "Anyone with the link" means anyone who gets the link can open it, even if it is forwarded to a stranger. For business files, such as proposals, pricing, customer lists, and anything with personal information, keep it Restricted and share with specific people. Use "Anyone with the link" only for things that are meant to be public, such as a menu or a flyer, and set it to Viewer.

You can open the Share window at any time to see who has access, change someone's level, or remove them. Do this when a project ends or an employee leaves.

### Automatic saving and version history

Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides save on their own as you type. A dead battery or a dropped connection does not cost you your work.

Version history, in the File menu, keeps every earlier state of the file. It shows who changed what and when, and it lets you restore an older version. If someone deletes a section by accident, you can bring it back. If a client says "that is not the price we agreed to," you can show what the file said on the day they approved it. Before an important moment, such as sending a proposal for signature, name the current version so you can go straight to it later. A named version called "Sent for signature" is the record that would have settled Malik's cabinet price in one minute.

### Share the folder, not file by file

A folder in Drive can be shared the same way a file can. Everything inside it carries the same access, and so does anything you add later. So in place of sharing twelve files one at a time, you make one folder and share it once.

Malik sets up his work this way. Each client gets a folder named with their last name and the job, such as "Harris, kitchen remodel." Inside are the proposal, the signed contract, photos, and invoices. He shares that folder with the client as a Commenter or Viewer. His bookkeeper gets Editor access to a separate folder that holds receipts and invoices for all jobs. One caution follows from this: before you put a file in a shared folder, check who can see that folder. Keep your private cost notes, supplier pricing, and markup somewhere else.

## Do this now

1. In Drive, create a folder for one real client or project and name it clearly.
2. Create or move one document into it, such as a quote or a proposal.
3. Share the folder with a neighbor in the session, or with a second email address of your own, as a Commenter.
4. Have them leave one comment and one suggested change.
5. Reply to the comment and accept or reject the suggestion.
6. Open version history and find the version from before their change.
7. Open the Share window, check the general access setting, and then change your neighbor to Viewer.

Finished result: one shared client folder with a live document, a resolved comment, and permissions you chose on purpose.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Editor access for everyone, because it is the fastest choice in the menu.** A price or a clause can change without your knowledge. Fix: start with the lowest level that works and raise it only if needed.
- **"Anyone with the link" on private files.** A forwarded link exposes pricing or customer details. Fix: keep business files Restricted and add people by email.
- **Downloading the file and emailing it anyway.** The copies start again. Fix: send the link, so there is still only one file.
- **Sharing files one at a time.** It takes longer, and something always gets missed. Fix: build a folder per client or project and share that.
- **Access that outlives the job.** Former staff and past clients can still open current files. Fix: review who has access when a job or a working relationship ends.

## Before you move on

- Open the Share window on your most important proposal or price list. Is every person on it at the lowest level that works?
- Which of your files are set to "Anyone with the link" right now, and should any be Restricted?
- Can you open version history on a document and restore yesterday's version in under a minute?
- Does one real client have a folder with everything for their job inside it?
- Is there anyone with access to your files who no longer works with you?

## What you walk away with

A client folder set up in Drive, a clear rule for choosing permissions, and one live file with full history in place of the pile of attachments.
