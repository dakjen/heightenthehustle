# One Page, One Link: Your Home Base for Every Profile

> Publish one page that says who you are, what you do, how to book, and where your reviews live, then put that one link on every profile you have.

## Why this matters

Your Google profile has a spot for a website. So do your social bios and your email signature. When each one points somewhere different, or nowhere, a customer who is ready to buy has to hunt for the next step. Take Denise, who runs a licensed home daycare. Her profiles were set up at different times over several years. Her Facebook page lists a phone number she has since changed, her Instagram has no link, and the website field on her Google profile is empty. A parent ready to enroll has to work to reach her, and many will pick the provider who made it quick. One page with the right information, linked from every place, fixes that in an afternoon.

## What you'll be able to do

- Point every profile at one page you control
- Decide whether a one-page site or a link page fits your business
- Write the four things the page must say
- Build and publish it in one sitting
- Place the link everywhere a customer might look

## The lesson

### One link for everything

Your home base is the single page you control that every other profile points to. Social media, Google, and email are the roads. All of them should lead to the same door.

One link pays off in two ways. The customer always lands somewhere current. And when something changes, such as your phone number or your booking method, you fix it in one spot and every profile is correct again.

### A full website can wait

A multi-page website costs money and takes weeks, and for most service businesses it is not what closes the sale. A customer deciding whether to call you wants four answers, and one page can give all four. Publish the one page now and let it start working. If the business later needs online ordering, a gallery, or a blog, you can grow into a bigger site then.

### The four things your page must say

1. **Who you are.** Your business name, your name, a photo of you or your work, and the area you serve.
2. **What you do.** Three to six services in plain words. Not everything you could ever do, only what you want to be hired for.
3. **How to book.** One clear action: call, text, email, or fill out a form. Pick the one you will actually answer and make it the biggest thing on the page.
4. **Your reviews link.** A button or line that sends people to your Google reviews, so they can see proof and happy customers can add more.

Here is how a finished page reads for a fictional business.

> **Little Sprouts Home Daycare**
> Licensed home child care in Capitol Heights, run by Denise Walker.
>
> **What we offer**
> - Full-day care, ages 6 weeks to 4 years
> - Before- and after-school care
> - Meals and snacks included
> - Small group, never more than eight children
>
> **How to enroll**
> Call or text Denise to schedule a visit. Visits are held weekday evenings after 6.
>
> **What parents say**
> Read our Google reviews [reviews link]

That is the whole page. A parent can read it in thirty seconds and knows exactly what to do next. Notice that it also answers the questions Denise would otherwise field by phone: ages, hours, meals, group size, and when visits happen. A good page cuts down the calls that go nowhere.

### Pick your tool

Three free options are named for this session. All of them work from a phone and none needs a designer.

| Tool | Best when | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Sites | You want a page that looks like a small website | A page with sections, photos, and buttons, made with your Google account |
| Canva | You care about looks and like working from templates | A designed one-page site built from a template |
| A link-in-bio page | You mostly send people to other places | A short list of buttons: call, book, reviews, social |

If you are undecided, pick the one you can finish today. A finished plain page beats an unfinished beautiful one.

### Build it in numbered steps

1. Write your four sections in a notes app or on paper before you open the tool. Words before design.
2. Open the tool you chose and start a new one-page site or link page.
3. Add your business name and one photo at the top.
4. Add your services as a short list.
5. Add your booking action as a button or a bold line with your phone number.
6. Add your reviews link.
7. Preview it on a phone, since that is how most customers will see it.
8. Publish, then copy the page link.

Before you publish, tap the booking action yourself. A phone number should start a call or a text when tapped, and a button should open the right place. Then save the page link in your phone's notes so you can paste it without looking it up.

### Use it everywhere

Once you have the link, paste it into every place that asks for a website: your Google Business Profile, your LinkedIn contact info, each social media bio, and your email signature. Put it on your next batch of business cards and flyers, and on your invoices and estimates. From now on, when someone asks "Do you have a website?" the answer is yes, and it is the same answer every time. One page, one link, used everywhere.

Put a reminder on your calendar to reread the page every few months. Prices, hours, and services shift, and the page is only worth linking to if it is current.

## Do this now

1. Write your four sections: who, what, how to book, reviews.
2. Choose Google Sites, Canva, or a link-in-bio page.
3. Build the page with your name, photo, services, booking action, and reviews link.
4. Check it on your phone and have a neighbor read it. Ask them, "What would you do next?" If they cannot answer, make the booking action bigger.
5. Publish it and copy the link.
6. Paste the link into your Google Business Profile, your LinkedIn, and one social bio before the session ends.

Finished result: one live page and one link, already placed on at least three of your profiles.

## Where owners lose time or money

- **Holding out for a full website.** Months pass with nothing to link to. Fix: publish the one page now and improve it later.
- **Listing every service you have ever done.** The work you want more of gets buried. Fix: keep it to the three to six you want to be hired for.
- **Three ways to book and no clear favorite.** Customers stall when they have to choose. Fix: pick one main action and make it stand out.
- **Different links in different places.** One of them is always out of date. Fix: replace them all with your one home base link.
- **A page checked only on a laptop.** Most customers see it on a phone, where a button may sit off screen. Fix: open your own link on your phone before you share it.

## Before you move on

- Can a stranger tell in thirty seconds what you do and how to hire you? Test it on a neighbor.
- Is there exactly one main booking action, and does it work when you tap it on your phone?
- Does the page link to your reviews?
- Where have you pasted the link so far, and which profiles, cards, or documents still need it?

## What you walk away with

A published one-page home base and a single link that now sits on your Google profile, your social bios, and soon your email signature.
